# Becoming a registered bookmaker in Victoria (2026): the full licensing pathway, solo and partnership routes, and realistic timelines

Research date: 9 October 2026. Jurisdiction: Victoria, Australia. Primary sources are the VGCCC's current (Feb/Mar 2025) application kits, FAQ and information sheet; the Racing Act 1958 (Vic) authorised consolidation; and the Victorian Bookmakers' Association (VBA) joining framework and eligibility policy effective 24 June 2025. Where a figure is an estimate or a secondary-source claim it is flagged.

A note on the legislation copy used: the consolidated Racing Act 1958 I could read in full was Authorised Version No. 177 (as at 31 December 2019) on legislation.vic.gov.au; AustLII blocked automated access. The VGCCC's 2025 material still cites the same section numbers (s 91, s 94A, s 4.5A.14/4.5A.17 of the Gambling Regulation Act 2003), so the structure appears unchanged, but readers should confirm against the current consolidation.

## Key Question 1: Who registers bookmakers in Victoria, and what is a "registered bookmaker" vs "key employee" vs "partnership" vs corporate registration? Can a company be registered?

### Takeaway
The Victorian Gambling and Casino Control Commission (VGCCC) registers bookmakers under Part 4.5A of the Gambling Regulation Act 2003, but registration alone does not let you trade: you also need a s 94A Racing Act bond (in practice VBA membership) and a club bookmaker's licence from Racing Victoria, Harness Racing Victoria or Greyhound Racing Victoria. Individuals (18+) and corporations can both be registered; a corporation must appoint a VGCCC-approved nominee; a "partnership" is a trading partnership of bookmakers who are each already registered; a "key employee" is a registered manager/decision-maker who cannot bookmake except as an approved substitute.

### Cited Findings
- "To be a bookmaker you must: be registered by the VGCCC; have appropriate bond arrangements under section 94A of the Racing Act 1958; and hold a club bookmaker's licence from the relevant racing industry body." — [VGCCC Bookmaker registration](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-registration)
- Statutory definition: "A bookmaker is a person: who (whether on their own account or as an employee or agent for another person) carries on the business or vocation of, or acts as a bookmaker or turf commission agent; who gains or endeavours to gain a livelihood entirely or partly by betting or making wagers." — [VGCCC FAQ – Bookmakers (Feb 2025)](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)
- "An individual aged 18 years or more, or a corporation may apply to the Victorian Gambling and Casino Control Commission (VGCCC) for registration as a bookmaker." — [VGCCC FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)
- Three VGCCC application kits exist, now lodged via the VGCCC Portal: Sole Trader (WAB051224), Partnership (WAB011224) and Corporation (WAB031224). "Partnership: all partners must first be approved sole trader bookmakers." — [VGCCC Bookmaker registration](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-registration); kits: [Sole trader](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB051224_Bookmaker-Registration-Sole-Trader_260225.pdf), [Partnership](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB011224_Bookmaker-registration-partnership_190225-%281%29.pdf), [Corporation](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB031224_Bookmaker-registration-Corporation_190225-%281%29.pdf)
- Submission channel conflict: the February 2025 kits say "Send application via email to: contact@vgccc.vic.gov.au", while the current web page says the forms "are now accessed via the VGCCC Portal". — [Sole trader kit](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB051224_Bookmaker-Registration-Sole-Trader_260225.pdf); [VGCCC Bookmaker registration](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-registration)
- Corporate registration and nominee: "The Act requires that if a corporation is approved as a registered bookmaker it must nominate a person to be approved by the VGCCC to be responsible as the nominee on behalf of the corporation. This ensures one person is accountable for the management and control of the bookmaking business, with the person nominated and approved to be liable under the Act as a registered bookmaker. The nominee application form must lodge at the same time as the registration as a bookmaker." — [VGCCC FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)
- Historical context: a 2010 Victorian Regulatory Impact Statement noted that "corporations have not previously been permitted to be registered as bookmakers (other than proprietary corporations on a strictly limited basis)" and that "since the early 1990s there have been no Government fees associated with applications for bookmaking related registrations." — [Gambling Regulation Amendment (Bookmaking Related Registration Fees) Regulations 2010 RIS](https://www.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2019-11/Gambling-Regulation-Amdnendment-Bookmaking-Related-Registration-Fees-Regulations-2010-RIS.pdf)
- "The VGCCC registers Victorian Bookmakers including partnerships and corporate entities. These organisations can only offer an online betting service if the co-regulator (e.g. Racing Victoria) issues an off-course permit." — [VGCCC Online wagering and sports betting providers](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/wagering-and-betting/online-wagering-and-sports-betting-providers)
- Partnership mechanics: "If you are a registered bookmaker and wish to enter into a partnership with another bookmaker, you must seek approval from the VGCCC to operate in a trading partnership with another registered bookmaker. All partners must be registered bookmakers." — [VGCCC FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)
- Key employee definition: "A bookmaker's key employee is a person who is: employed by a bookmaker in a managerial role; authorised to make decisions relevant to business operations of a bookmaker." Applicants "must be at least 18 years of age and must not have been convicted of a 'relevant offence' in the last 10 years." Registration lasts up to 10 years. — [VGCCC Bookmaker key employee](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-key-employee); [Key employee kit WAB041224](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-12/WAB041224_Bookmaker-Key-Employee.pdf)
- Key employee limits: "An employee not registered as a 'key employee' cannot perform the duties of a bookmaker. Furthermore, a key employee may not perform the duties of a bookmaker, unless they have been approved as a substitute by Racing Victoria Limited, Harness Racing Victoria or Greyhound Racing Victoria. Bookmakers may perform any of the duties of a key employee except they are not eligible to be licensed as an approved substitute for another bookmaker." — [VGCCC FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)
- Approved substitute (statute): "A registered bookmaker may apply to a controlling body for approval of a registered bookmaker's key employee to carry on the business of the registered bookmaker during a period of absence"; each controlling body keeps a Register of Approved Substitutes; "An approved substitute is subject to the provisions of this Act ... as if the approved substitute were a registered bookmaker." — Racing Act 1958 s 91AB, [authorised consolidation](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/723edbc0-2ae6-3f51-bf06-3e51623bda11_58-6353aa177%20authorised.pdf)
- Offence to bookmake without both approvals: s 91(1): "A person must not carry on the business of bookmaking on a racecourse, part of a racecourse, an approved off-course premises or a sports ground unless the person (a) is a registered bookmaker or an approved substitute; and (b) in the case of a registered bookmaker, holds a club bookmaker's licence ... if such a licence is required by a controlling body". Penalty: 1 year imprisonment or 120 penalty units or both. s 91(4): must not employ a person as a bookmaker's key employee unless they are registered (20 penalty units). — [Racing Act 1958 s 91](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/723edbc0-2ae6-3f51-bf06-3e51623bda11_58-6353aa177%20authorised.pdf)
- Associates of a registered bookmaker must also be approved: directors, executive officers, 10%+ shareholders, 10%+ partners, trustees/beneficiaries with control, and "any associate who could exercise a significant influence over ... the bookmaking business". — [VGCCC FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)
- Review rights: refusal of registration, refusal of partnership approval, refusal of nominee approval, conditions, non-renewal and disciplinary action are all reviewable at VCAT. — [VGCCC FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf); [VCAT – Racing Act 1958 review](https://www.vcat.vic.gov.au/case-types/review-and-regulation/application-for-review-of-a-decision/racing-act-1958-review)

### Inferences
- "Registered bookmaker" is the VGCCC probity/identity layer; "club bookmaker's licence" (Racing Victoria etc.) is the commercial authority to field; the VBA bond is the consumer-protection layer. All three are needed before the first bet, which is why the process is sequential and slow.
- A new entrant who wants to trade through a company can apply as a corporation with themself as nominee and associate, but the nominee is personally liable "as a registered bookmaker", so the corporate wrapper does not reduce personal probity scrutiny.
- A partnership is not a shortcut for a new entrant: every partner must already be a registered sole-trader bookmaker before the partnership can be approved.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the full text of Part 4.5A of the Gambling Regulation Act 2003 (grounds for refusal, "suitable person" test wording). The VGCCC pages confirm s 4.5A.14 (suspension/cancellation) and s 4.5A.17 (relevant-offence employment ban) exist.

## Key Question 2: Eligibility and probity requirements (age, residency, police checks, finances, references, interviews, exams/training, bankruptcy)

### Takeaway
VGCCC probity is document-heavy rather than exam-based: 18+, National Police Certificate, credit report, ASIC extract, three years of tax returns, full statement of assets/liabilities/net worth and five-year source of funds, family and associate disclosure, business plan, and a compliant Responsible Gambling Code. There is no VGCCC written exam, interview requirement or minimum net worth published. The real financial and competence hurdles sit with the VBA, whose 2025 framework requires a formal Board interview, demonstrated knowledge of the rules, prior industry experience (100 race meetings, or 3 years corporate for the Internet class) and minimum net assets of $250,000 to $1,000,000 depending on class.

### Cited Findings
**VGCCC layer**
- Age/offence bar: "Applicants must be at least 18 years of age and must not have been convicted of a 'relevant offence' in the last 10 years." — [VGCCC Bookmakers – application process](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/gambling/bookmaker-and-employee/apply-new-licence/application-process-and-requirements)
- Relevant offence definition (VGCCC summary): "an offence against the Racing Act 1958, or a gaming Act or regulations; an offence involving fraud or dishonesty; an indictable offence or an offence that, if committed in Victoria, would be an indictable offence." — [VGCCC Information for a registered bookmaker (Feb 2025)](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Gambling-information-sheet-Information-for-a-registered-bookmaker-(2).pdf)
- Required documents for individuals: ASIC current and historical personal name extract; National Police Certificate "no more than 3 months from date of issue"; credit report (Equifax, illion or Experian; "not a credit score"); passport-style photo taken within 3 months; adopted Code of Conduct. Overseas arrivals within 3 months must supply overseas and Australian police checks and credit reports. — [VGCCC Bookmaker registration](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-registration)
- Sole trader additional: financial institution letter; "Tax returns and Notice of Assessment from the Australian Tax Office of the past 3 financial years"; court outcome/settlement documents; "Evidence of your VBA Bond or other guarantee"; associated individual/entity forms; "Audited and certified financial statements"; "A copy of the business plan including financial projections for the first 3 years and governance and compliance framework, experience in managing a business, methods of accepting gambling payments and proportions (cash etc) and where and how the business is to operate." — [Sole trader kit WAB051224 p.2](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB051224_Bookmaker-Registration-Sole-Trader_260225.pdf)
- The sole trader form asks the applicant to tick whether the application is for "On course", "Online" or "Both" (Q1), and asks for "Estimated gross turnover for the next financial year", "Intended race meetings", "Bet types", "Telephone usage" and "Workload rosters" (Q18). — [Sole trader kit](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB051224_Bookmaker-Registration-Sole-Trader_260225.pdf)
- Police check: "National name check" National Police Certificate from Victoria Police; "Finger and palm prints are not required" for bookmaker/nominee applicants. The kit says the NPC "cannot be a copy and must be obtained not more than 3 months before this form is lodged", and spent convictions, diversion programs, good-behaviour bonds and matters with no conviction recorded must still be disclosed at Q28; "Children's Court matters more than 10 years old and non-custodial traffic matters" are exempt. — [VGCCC Bookmaker registration](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-registration); [Sole trader kit p.12](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB051224_Bookmaker-Registration-Sole-Trader_260225.pdf)
- Residency: if you have lived outside Australia in the last 10 years you must obtain an equivalent overseas police report and credit report; the form asks "No. of years resident in this country" and requires 10 years of address history. No citizenship or Victorian residency requirement is stated. — [Sole trader kit pp.4, 12-13](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB051224_Bookmaker-Registration-Sole-Trader_260225.pdf)
- Family disclosure: the form requires names, dates of birth and addresses of the applicant's partner, parents and parents-in-law, siblings and siblings-in-law, and children/step-children (Q10-Q13), and whether any family member will work in the business (Q14). — [Sole trader kit pp.4-8](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB051224_Bookmaker-Registration-Sole-Trader_260225.pdf)
- Bankruptcy/financial questions: Q30 "have you personally ever been subject to bankruptcy or any insolvency arrangements?"; Q31 officer of a wound-up/liquidated entity; Q32 personal guarantor for others' debts; Q34 "Are you in default of any debt repayment or loan (including less than $5,000)?". A full statement of assets and liabilities with "NET WORTH (total assets less total liabilities)" and a five-year "Source of funds" table (Schedules A-L) is required. — [Sole trader kit pp.13-16](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB051224_Bookmaker-Registration-Sole-Trader_260225.pdf)
- Insolvency is a disciplinary ground after registration: "the registration holder has become an insolvent under administration". — [VGCCC Information sheet](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Gambling-information-sheet-Information-for-a-registered-bookmaker-(2).pdf)
- Other probity questions: legal actions in past 10 years (Q20-21); prior racing/gambling licence applications anywhere (Q22); exclusions from casinos/racecourses/online providers (Q22c); investigations by ASIC/APRA/ACCC etc (Q23); disciplinary/enforcement history (Q24); "full regulatory, breach and compliance history with the gambling regulators other than the VGCCC over the last five years" (Q29); declaration "I have not had my employment terminated for misconduct in the last 10 years". — [Sole trader kit pp.9-15](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB051224_Bookmaker-Registration-Sole-Trader_260225.pdf)
- Consent: "The applicant hereby consents to all probity investigations carried out by the Victorian Gambling and Casino Control Commission" including release of information by law enforcement agencies and financial institutions. — [Sole trader kit p.25](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB051224_Bookmaker-Registration-Sole-Trader_260225.pdf)
- Responsible Gambling Code: "The VGCCC will not approve a registration without a compliant code and your application will not be finalised until a code is lodged." The form's default is "Name of code: Victorian Bookmakers' Association Responsible Gambling Code of Conduct". — [Sole trader kit pp.15, 27](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB051224_Bookmaker-Registration-Sole-Trader_260225.pdf). The VBA code (version 1.05, effective 1 January 2026) is published by the VGCCC. — [VBA Code of Conduct (On-course, telephone and online) 1 Jan 2026](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-12/VBA-Code-of-Conduct-(On-course-telephone-and-online)-1-January-2026.pdf)
- False information: "Your application may be refused, and you may be prosecuted and fined up to 60 penalty units." — [VGCCC Bookmaker registration](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-registration)

**VBA layer (the de facto competence and capital test)**
- Application must include "employment records (letters of record of employment from past and present employers) in support of relevant work / business experience in bookmaking", a statutory declaration of ownership structure, "confirmation of sufficient funds availability to subscribe to the maximum number of Joint & Several Guarantee Units (19) and additional security", a 3-year business plan, "a Federal Police check certificate", a credit report, and the applicant must "Make themselves available for a formal interview with the VBA Board". — [VBA Joining Criteria & Fee Framework (effective 24 June 2025)](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Joining-Criteria-Fee-Framework-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)
- Knowledge test (not a written exam): applicant must "Demonstrate to the Board's satisfaction a sound knowledge of the commercial and regulatory environment in which a bookmaker operates, including: AML / CTF laws, the Rules of Racing (Vic), Club Bookmaker' Licence Rules, Bookmakers' Sports Telephone and Internet Rules (including Harness and Greyhound), Approved Bet Types, and VBA Bookmaker Policies (including notably the Responsible Gambling Code of Conduct)." — [VBA Joining Criteria](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Joining-Criteria-Fee-Framework-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)
- VBA class table (age / experience / minimum qualifying net assets / joining fee ex-GST):
  - Class I (Internet): 25+; "3-5 years corporate employment" with a licensed bookmaker in middle-to-senior management across phone ops, trading, analytics, BD, affiliate management, disputes, raceday ops, responsible gambling, AML/CTF; net assets "$1,000,000, of which at least $250,000 must be cash at bank and/or cash equivalents"; joining fee $5,000.
  - Class R (Rails, metro): 25+; 100 race meetings as key employee/clerk; $750,000 (of which $150,000 cash); $1,500.
  - Class O (Other/Paddock, metro): 21+; 100 race meetings; $500,000 ($100,000 cash); $1,500.
  - Class C (Country): 21+; 100 race meetings; $250,000 ($50,000 cash); $1,500.
  - Class F (Family): 18+; 50 race meetings; no net asset test; $750; must partner/incorporate with an existing VBA member related by blood or marriage. — [VBA Joining Criteria](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Joining-Criteria-Fee-Framework-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf); [VBA Membership Eligibility Policy v2025.2](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Membership-Eligibility-Policy-and-Terms-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)
- Asset rules: cash "must be held in the Applicant's name for at least six (6) months prior to the date recorded on the VBA Membership Application Form"; trusts, unvested super, vehicles and furniture excluded; only Bitcoin and Ethereum counted, at 25% of market value. — [VBA Joining Criteria note 4](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Joining-Criteria-Fee-Framework-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)
- Board discretion: the VBA "may determine, in its absolute discretion and without providing any reasons, to either approve ... reject ... or impose conditions", and "may approve the membership of any person to any Membership Class in its absolute discretion, including in circumstances where the Applicant would not otherwise meet the Eligibility Criteria". The Board may also "reduce the required number of meetings' experience". — [VBA Eligibility Policy cl 5.4, 5.8](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Membership-Eligibility-Policy-and-Terms-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf); [VBA Joining Criteria note 1](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Joining-Criteria-Fee-Framework-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)
- Eligibility precondition: "To be eligible for membership with the Association, an Applicant must: (a) be a bookmaker registered in the State of Victoria by the VGCCC; (b) be of good character, of sound financial position and of good business reputation ...; (c) at least twenty-one (21) business days before commencing its operations as a Bookmaker, provide the Association with the Applicant's Policy Documents and Business Plan". — [VBA Eligibility Policy cl 10.1](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Membership-Eligibility-Policy-and-Terms-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)
- Medical: no medical examination requirement appears in either the VGCCC kits or the VBA policy; the VBA policy only provides that membership ceases where an individual "is experiencing an impairment in mental health or illness" per its Constitution cl 16(a)(iv). — [VBA Eligibility Policy cl 9.1(d)](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Membership-Eligibility-Policy-and-Terms-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- There is a circularity the applicant must manage: the VGCCC wants "Evidence of your VBA Bond or other guarantee" with the application and "cannot determine a bookmaker's application until satisfied that the applicant has appropriate bond arrangements", while the VBA requires the applicant to "be a bookmaker registered ... by the VGCCC". In practice this appears to be resolved by the VBA providing written confirmation of in-principle guarantee status (the partnership kit asks for "written confirmation from the Victorian Bookmakers' Association Ltd of the default guarantee status of the proposed members"), with membership taking effect once the bond is paid after VGCCC approval. Applicants should approach the VBA first or in parallel.
- For a person "starting cold" with no bookmaking employment history, the VBA experience thresholds (100 race meetings as a clerk/key employee, or 3+ years in a corporate bookmaker) are the binding constraint, not VGCCC probity. The only published way around them is Board discretion, the family class, or buying into an existing business (see Q6).

### Gaps
- No source states a minimum net worth for VGCCC registration itself (the kit collects net worth but publishes no threshold); the thresholds found are the VBA's.
- No VGCCC reference-letter or interview requirement was found; the VBA interview is the only interview requirement located.
- No formal course or written exam (e.g. a VBA education module) was found in current documents; the "sound knowledge" test is assessed at interview.

## Key Question 3: Step-by-step process, forms, fees, bond/guarantee, VGCCC assessment time and publication

### Takeaway
VGCCC registration carries no government application fee for individuals (ABLIS: "No fees are required"; the VGCCC fee schedule from 1 July 2026 lists no bookmaker line), lasts 10 years, and has no published processing time ("Each application is different"); the VGCCC's generic guidance is to lodge "at least eight weeks in advance". The bond is satisfied by joining the VBA guarantee fund (joining fee $750-$5,000 ex-GST plus a Base Guarantee and up to 19 "Joint & Several Guarantee Units", amounts not published) or by alternative arrangements approved by the VGCCC. Decisions are published on the VGCCC website within 14 days.

### Cited Findings
- Sequence: "Applicants must first register as a bookmaker with the VGCCC before obtaining a licence from either of the racing industry controlling bodies. You must not undertake any duties as a bookmaker without having both your registration and licence approved." — [VGCCC FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)
- Racing Victoria side: "Racing Victoria will not accept any application unless and until you have been registered as a bookmaker by the VGCCC." — Racing Victoria application form (2024-25 licence term), via [search summary of racingvictoria.com.au form](https://racingvictoria.com.au/wagering/-/media/d64e9fef637a440d937a60b02cb16048.ashx) (file could not be opened directly; 404/blocked at time of research)
- Forms: "These forms are to be completed online, and then printed for signature ... incorrect or incomplete applications (including relevant documentation) will not be accepted, and will be returned to the applicant." — [VGCCC FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)
- Fees (VGCCC): the ABLIS entry for "Registration as a Bookmaker – Victoria" states "Fees: No fees are required", duration "10 years", and lists the governing instruments as Gambling Regulation Act 2003, Racing Act 1958 and "Gambling Regulations 2026 (Victoria)". — [ABLIS – Registration as a Bookmaker VIC](https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/vic/registration-as-a-bookmaker/23992)
- The VGCCC application fees table "from 1 July 2026" (page updated 1 July 2026) contains no bookmaker registration, renewal, key employee or nominee line; associate fees are $283.60 (natural person) and $1,592.80 (other), but "do not apply when the associate application is lodged with a new gambling industry participant licence" or with a registered bookmaker renewal. — [VGCCC Application fees](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/resources-hub/application-fees)
- Historical corporate fee: the 2010 RIS proposed 461.85 fee units ($5,520 then) for corporate applicants and 237.81 fee units ($2,843) for corporate renewal, with nil for natural persons. Whether a corporate fee is currently charged could not be confirmed from the 2026 fee page. — [2010 RIS](https://www.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2019-11/Gambling-Regulation-Amdnendment-Bookmaking-Related-Registration-Fees-Regulations-2010-RIS.pdf)
- Processing time (official): "How long does the registration process take? Each application is different ... You will be sent a letter that confirms we have received your application and outlines any further information required. Once we have received all the required information and documents, your application will be determined. You will be advised of the outcome in writing. Decisions are also published on the VGCCC website within 14 days." — [VGCCC FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)
- Generic VGCCC guidance: "Please lodge your application (other than a Casino Special Employee (CSE) renewal) at least eight weeks in advance" and "The time taken to process an application will vary depending on a range of factors including the complexity of the application and any objections which may be raised." — [VGCCC Application fees](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/resources-hub/application-fees)
- No public objection period for bookmaker registration was found; the "objections" reference above is generic to gaming licences. Decisions are published post hoc on the [Approved bookmakers list](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/approved-bookmakers-report) (linked from the registration page). — [VGCCC Bookmaker registration](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-registration)
- Duty during assessment: "You must notify the VGCCC in writing immediately of any changes to information provided ... until a decision is made." — [VGCCC Bookmaker registration](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-registration)
- Bond (statute): s 94A(1) "The Commission, with the approval of the Minister, may (a) arrange with the Victorian Bookmakers Association Limited ACN 004 236 677 (the Company) for the Company to lodge with the Commission a bond in the determined amount ... to satisfy defaults made by registered bookmakers or their approved substitutes in the payment of wagers ...; or (b) require a registered bookmaker to make arrangements satisfactory to the Commission for the lodgement of a bond in the determined amount". The "determined amount" is set by Order under s 94A(2B) per class of bookmaker/wager. — [Racing Act 1958 s 94A](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/723edbc0-2ae6-3f51-bf06-3e51623bda11_58-6353aa177%20authorised.pdf)
- VGCCC on bond: "the guarantee (bond) can be arranged between the applicant and the Victorian Bookmakers' Association in the determined amount ... Alternative arrangements can be made as long as the VGCCC has approved. Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2003 (the Act), the VGCCC cannot determine a bookmaker's application until satisfied that the applicant has appropriate bond arrangements." — [VGCCC FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)
- VBA guarantee mechanics: "every member must deposit with the Association such amount or security as is determined by the Association to be maintained in funds established for the purposes of satisfying claims made against it under section 94A"; the amount "varies depending on ... the Membership Class"; security may be "mortgage or cash"; the VBA "may require a member to deposit ... additional amounts or securities" for customer deposits. — [VBA Eligibility Policy cl 8](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Membership-Eligibility-Policy-and-Terms-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)
- VBA joining costs: Joining Fee ($5,000 Class I; $1,500 R/O/C; $750 F; ex-GST; 50% discount if related to an existing member), "payable immediately upon VBA Board approval ... and is non-refundable", plus "Base Guarantee amounts" and the need to show funds for "the maximum number of Joint & Several Guarantee Units (19)". Dollar values of the Base Guarantee and units are not published. — [VBA Joining Criteria notes 7-8](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Joining-Criteria-Fee-Framework-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)
- Class I (internet) extra security: for at least two years from first operating, "deposit with the VBA a $50,000 Customer Deposit Surety", deliver a monthly (then quarterly) "Customer Deposits Ratio Certificate" showing bank cash to customer deposits "of not less than 80% at all times", and have website terms and conditions approved by the VBA before launch. — [VBA Joining Criteria note 7](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Joining-Criteria-Fee-Framework-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)
- What the VBA guarantee covers for punters: defaults in payment of wagers (60-day claim window for cash/account bets, 21 days for credit bets); it "does not extend to claims over wagering deposits held by a bookmaker". — [VBA Guarantee](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/vba-guarantee/)
- Annual VBA costs: an "Annual Subscription Fee ... in an amount as determined by the Board" plus any "management or administrative fees". Amounts not published. — [VBA Eligibility Policy cl 2, 4.2](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Membership-Eligibility-Policy-and-Terms-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)
- Outputs on approval: "the VGCCC issues a certificate of registration and an identity card"; registration "lasts 10 years"; renewal reminder sent 3 months before expiry; late renewal "can result in cancelled registration". — [VGCCC Bookmaker registration](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-registration)
- Post-registration directions: must notify the VGCCC in writing within 14 days of specified changes (address, name, charges, bankruptcy etc.); failure is a 60-penalty-unit offence. — [VGCCC Ministerial directions for bookmakers](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/ministerial-directions-bookmakers); [VGCCC FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)

### Inferences
- Minimum out-of-pocket to get registered as an individual appears to be the cost of the NPC, credit report, ASIC extract, audited financial statements/accountant, business plan, plus the VBA joining fee and guarantee deposits. The guarantee deposit is the large unknown; the VBA requires proof of funds for 19 guarantee units plus extra security, which suggests a five- to six-figure commitment for the Internet class, but no dollar figure is published.
- "Eight weeks" is a floor, not a forecast: the FAQ's "each application is different" and the requirement that the bond be in place before determination mean the VGCCC clock effectively runs in parallel with (and can be gated by) the VBA process.

### Gaps
- The s 94A(2B) Order setting the "determined amount" of the bond per class could not be located; the VBA's Base Guarantee and guarantee-unit dollar values are unpublished.
- Whether a corporate applicant currently pays a VGCCC registration fee (2010 RIS proposed one; 2026 fee page lists none).
- The VGCCC publishes no bookmaker-specific processing statistics.

## Key Question 4: After VGCCC registration, what else is needed to actually trade (VBA, racing body licence, off-course/online approval, stands, attendance obligations, sports betting, interstate customers)?

### Takeaway
After registration you need: (1) VBA membership (bond); (2) a club bookmaker's licence from Racing Victoria (and/or HRV, GRV), which involves a 13-step application covering area allocation/venues, modes of betting and betting systems, a financial statement and an off-course premises form; (3) for phone/internet betting, approval under Racing Act ss 4A-4E of an "approved off-course premises" (which must not be open to the public) and compliance with Racing Victoria's Telephone and Internet Betting Rules; (4) VGCCC approval of any non-racing betting event plus a Product Fee and Integrity Agreement with each approved Sports Controlling Body; and (5) NCPF/AML compliance. The VBA itself locks new Internet-class members to online/telephone only for two years and on-course classes to on-course/telephone only.

### Cited Findings
- Club licence role: "Licences provide the authority for bookmakers to field at race meetings of each racing code and in some cases, to conduct 24-hour trading at racecourses." Each body has its own rules: Racing Victoria "rules of racing for licensed bookmakers"; HRV "Australian Harness Racing Rules"; GRV "Betting and Bookmakers Rules". — [VGCCC FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)
- Where you may trade: registration plus licence covers "a Victorian racecourse, part of a racecourse, an approved off-course premises or a sports ground"; penalty for trading without both is "up to one year imprisonment, a fine of 120 penalty units, or both". — [VGCCC Information sheet](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Gambling-information-sheet-Information-for-a-registered-bookmaker-(2).pdf)
- Racing Victoria contacts/process: Wagering Licensing & Regulation Department, +61 3 9258 4667, b.admin@racingvictoria.net.au, 400 Epsom Road Flemington; the application form "guides applicants through 13 steps ... application type, partnership or individual details, area allocation and venues, modes of betting, and betting systems ... declarations, supporting documents, ... a Financial Statement Form and, where relevant, an Off-course Premises Form". The 2024-25 form set a 28 June deadline and "A fee of $100 will apply to bookmakers who provide these documents after this date"; licence term "1 August 2024 – 31 July 2025". — Racing Victoria application form via [search summary](https://racingvictoria.com.au/wagering/-/media/d64e9fef637a440d937a60b02cb16048.ashx) (direct download blocked)
- Rules: Racing Victoria's "Club Bookmakers' Licence Rules" (version 2024.2, approved under s 91D) "cover how Racing Victoria may grant a licence, the approval process, limitations, and the factors it considers"; separate "Bookmakers' Licence Levy Rules 2012" exist. — [Club Bookmakers' Licence Rules](https://racingvictoria.com.au/wagering/-/media/ab7b828f442f4c1ebf14b844c599c02c.ashx) and [Levy Rules](https://racingvictoria.com.au/wagering/~/-/media/71d4bb2876a442b9ba46c842c8b16e2b.ashx) (both via search summaries; files returned 404 on fetch)
- Levy: "Controlling bodies may also impose a monetary licence levy on registered bookmakers who hold a club bookmaker's licence with them. The levy imposed must be approved by the Minister for Racing." — [VGCCC Information sheet](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Gambling-information-sheet-Information-for-a-registered-bookmaker-(2).pdf); Racing Act s 91B "Bookmaker's licence levy" and s 91C "Bookmaking development fund". — [Racing Act 1958](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/723edbc0-2ae6-3f51-bf06-3e51623bda11_58-6353aa177%20authorised.pdf)
- Online/phone legal basis: s 4(3)-(4) Racing Act: the bet must be made with a registered bookmaker or approved substitute "who is present on the racecourse or approved off-course premises at the time the bet is made", either during a race meeting on-course or "at any time by a person using a method of [remote betting approved under s 4A]". s 4A: Minister gazettes approved methods of communication. s 4B: "A registered bookmaker issued with a club bookmaker's licence may apply to the controlling body that issued the licence, for approval to make or accept bets at premises other than a racecourse". s 4C: controlling body may approve "premises in Victoria that are not on a racecourse"; mandatory condition "that the approved off-course premises not be open to, or available for use by, the public"; refusal appealable to the Victorian Racing Tribunal; VGCCC must be advised. s 4E: Minister may gazette requirements bookmakers must meet before off-course approval. s 4G-4H: offences for breaching conditions or taking bets from persons present at the premises (up to 1000 penalty units / 2 years for repeat). — [Racing Act 1958 ss 4-4H](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/723edbc0-2ae6-3f51-bf06-3e51623bda11_58-6353aa177%20authorised.pdf)
- VGCCC on online: "Approved Victorian bookmakers can offer wagering markets on a racecourse and off-course (online) markets if approved by Racing Victoria and the Victorian Gambling and Casino Control Commission (VGCCC)." — [VGCCC Wagering and betting](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/wagering-and-betting)
- Rule sets a Victorian bookmaker must follow (listed in the VBA code): "Club Bookmakers' Licence Rules; Bookmaker Sports Betting Rules; Bookmakers' Telephone Betting Rules; Bookmakers' Internet Betting Rules" (Racing Victoria), HRV's Bookmakers and Betting Rules, GRV's Betting and Bookmaker Rules. VBA members "operate their bookmaking businesses ... in respect of some VBA Members, also over the telephone and through digital channels (internet)". — [VBA Code of Conduct 1 Jan 2026](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-12/VBA-Code-of-Conduct-(On-course-telephone-and-online)-1-January-2026.pdf)
- VBA class restrictions after joining: "A newly approved Member under Classes R, O, C, and F shall be permitted to operate only as an oncourse / telephone bookmaker for an initial period of two (2) years ... A newly approved Member under Class I shall be permitted to operate only as an online / telephone bookmaker for the Initial Period." — [VBA Joining Criteria note 6](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Joining-Criteria-Fee-Framework-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)
- Stand allocation: "Historically, Applicants would typically apply for a basic Membership and then seek working opportunities through the city and country allocation ballot systems." — [VBA Joining Criteria note 9](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Joining-Criteria-Fee-Framework-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf). The VBA says it has secured "permission to establish offcourse or home offices for digital businesses" and "digital wagering turnover now exceeds oncourse turnover". — [VBA Our History](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/our-history/)
- Sports/other events: "For a bookmaker to take bets on an event other than horseracing, harness racing or greyhound racing, you will need to obtain approval for a betting contingency from the VGCCC." — [VGCCC Information sheet](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Gambling-information-sheet-Information-for-a-registered-bookmaker-(2).pdf). Steps: confirm the event is on the approved betting events list; if the sport has an approved Sports Controlling Body, "the WSP must have an integrity agreement, called a Product Fee and Integrity Agreement (PFIA), in place"; offering markets without one breaches s 4.5.22 (120 penalty units); disputes can go to the Commission under s 4.5.26. — [VGCCC Online wagering and sports betting providers](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/wagering-and-betting/online-wagering-and-sports-betting-providers); [VGCCC Approved betting events](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/gambling/wagering-and-sports-betting/about-wagering-and-sports-betting/approved-betting-events)
- Consumer-protection obligations for online: NCPF (inducement limits, account closure, pre-commitment, activity statements, messaging, staff training), harm-minimisation ministerial directions, s 4.5.29, no sign-up inducements, no static betting advertising (s 4.7.1). — [VGCCC Online wagering and sports betting providers](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/wagering-and-betting/online-wagering-and-sports-betting-providers)
- Enforcement example for a Victorian-registered online bookmaker: VicBet (Rod Cleary; on-course and internet, est. 1992) fined A$130,000 in January 2025 for offering a bonus bet after an account-closure request and marketing to a self-excluded customer. — [iGB – Victoria fines VicBet](https://igamingbusiness.com/legal-compliance/victoria-fines-vicbet-care-failures/); [Bureau of Bookmaking – Victoria](https://bureauofbookmaking.com.au/vic/high-limit-mbl)
- Interstate customers: online wagering is lawful nationally when the provider "holds a licence from an Australian state or territory"; the VGCCC monitors all WSPs serving Victorians. Point-of-consumption tax is "applied as a percentage of the net wagering revenue derived from all wagering and betting activity by customers in the relevant jurisdiction, not the State or Territory where the bookmaker is licensed". — [VGCCC Online wagering and sports betting providers](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/wagering-and-betting/online-wagering-and-sports-betting-providers); POCT quote via [search summary](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/wagering-and-betting)
- Minimum bet limits: Racing Victoria's MBL policy applies to approved WSPs on Victorian thoroughbred races "and will include telephone and internet bets ... but exclude bets placed in a retail outlet". — [Racing Victoria Minimum Bet Limit](https://racingvictoria.com.au/wagering/minimum-bet-limit)
- AML: Victorian bookmakers taking phone/online bets are AML/CTF reporting entities (the VBA code references the AML/CTF Act 2006 and Rules); federal AML/CTF reforms "will take effect in 2026 impacting ... wagering service providers". — [VBA Code of Conduct](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-12/VBA-Code-of-Conduct-(On-course-telephone-and-online)-1-January-2026.pdf); [Senet – VGCCC plans 2025-26](https://www.senetgroup.com/news-and-insights/victorian-gambling-regulator-vgccc-shares-plans-for-2025-26)

### Inferences
- The racing-body licence year runs 1 August to 31 July with an end-June document deadline, so an applicant who misses the June window may be fitted in mid-year but should plan around that cycle.
- For sports betting, each sport's PFIA is a separate commercial negotiation (and fee), so a launch offering racing plus a few major sports needs several agreements in place, each of which can take weeks.
- Interstate racing product: betting on NSW/QLD etc. races requires race-fields approvals from each interstate PRA (not researched here but standard for all Australian WSPs; Senet's guide notes product fee/race fields as a step).

### Gaps
- I could not open Racing Victoria's Club Bookmakers' Licence Rules, Telephone Betting Rules, Internet Betting Rules or the current (2025-26 or 2026-27) application form; the fee/levy amounts and any minimum fielding/attendance requirement for on-course licences are therefore unconfirmed. No source found states a minimum number of race-meeting attendances; the VBA's two-year "online/telephone only" restriction for Class I implies none for that class.
- The ministerial requirements gazetted under s 4E (e.g. system/premises standards for off-course approval) were not located.

## Key Question 5: Can a Victorian registered bookmaker run a purely online book from an office without attending racecourses, and how does this compare with the NT route?

### Takeaway
Yes: Victoria's 2012 amendments (Racing Act ss 4A-4H) let a licensed Victorian bookmaker accept phone/internet bets from "approved off-course premises" (an office or home office, closed to the public), and the VBA now has a dedicated "Class I – Internet" membership that is confined to online/telephone for its first two years. NT's corporate bookmaker licence remains the mainstream route for scalable online operators (no cap on licences, built for corporates), but it costs about A$29,000 per year in fees, a $200,000-$250,000 bank guarantee and roughly six months to obtain; Victoria has no government fee for an individual but carries VBA capital tests ($1m net assets for Internet class) and a two-tier approval.

### Cited Findings
**Victoria online**
- VBA Class I membership is "members who are registered to carry on the business of a bookmaker over the internet". Age 25+; corporate bookmaking experience 3+ years within last 7; net assets $1,000,000 incl. $250,000 cash; joining fee $5,000. — [VBA Eligibility Policy cl 2, 13](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Membership-Eligibility-Policy-and-Terms-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf); [VBA Joining Criteria](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Joining-Criteria-Fee-Framework-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)
- "A newly approved Member under Class I shall be permitted to operate only as an online / telephone bookmaker for the Initial Period [two years]." — [VBA Joining Criteria note 6](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Joining-Criteria-Fee-Framework-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)
- The VGCCC sole trader form's first question lets the applicant choose "On course / Online / Both". — [Sole trader kit Q1](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB051224_Bookmaker-Registration-Sole-Trader_260225.pdf)
- VBA: "permission to establish offcourse or home offices for digital businesses" is listed among its achievements; "digital wagering turnover now exceeds oncourse turnover". — [VBA Our History](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/our-history/)
- Statutory requirement that the bookmaker or substitute be "present on the ... approved off-course premises at the time the bet is made" and that the premises "not be open to ... the public". — [Racing Act ss 4(3), 4C(5)](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/723edbc0-2ae6-3f51-bf06-3e51623bda11_58-6353aa177%20authorised.pdf)
- Victorian regulatory framing of the market: "Most online corporate bookmakers serving Victorian residents are licensed by the Northern Territory Racing and Wagering Commission, though they take bets Australia-wide." — [Bureau of Bookmaking – Victoria](https://bureauofbookmaking.com.au/vic/high-limit-mbl)
- Victoria's 2024 Tabcorp licence was designed to produce "a level playing field in Victoria", with Tabcorp "paying wagering taxes and racing and sports product fees on the same basis as other wagering operators", explicitly responding to "the proliferation of online bookmakers licensed in the Northern Territory". — [AGB – Tabcorp wins 20-year Victorian licence](https://asgam.com/2023/12/18/tabcorp-wins-new-20-year-victorian-wagering-and-betting-license/)

**Northern Territory comparison**
- "There is no limit on the number of Corporate Bookmaker Licences the NT may issue"; applicant "must be a corporate entity registered under the Corporations Act 2001"; assessment covers ownership, financial resources, business ability and probity of directors/associates; must propose "net economic benefit" to the NT (payments, sponsorships, local presence, traineeships); documents include probity deeds for directors and 10%+ shareholders, betting rules, AML/CTF program, 3-year business plan and Internal Control System. "The licensing process typically takes around 6 months from application." Bank guarantee "of at least $200,000 ... regulatory maximum is $250,000". NT tax: 10% of gross profit on racing; nil on domestic sports; combined cap $605,000 or 500,000 revenue units. — [Senet – Guide to obtaining an online bookmaker licence in Australia](https://www.senetgroup.com/news-and-insights/diversifying-into-new-markets-a-guide-to-obtaining-an-online-bookmaker-licence-in-australia/9)
- NT fees: application, renewal and annual fee each 20,000 revenue units (Racing and Wagering Regulations 2024); ABLIS lists the 2025-26 application fee as $29,000; licence term up to 20 years; "Your application won't be reviewed until the fee has been received"; cannot operate until the Commission confirms the $250,000 bank guarantee, system approval, fingerprint checks, "appointing a local nominee, and inspection of local premises". — [NT Bookmaker licence fees](https://nt.gov.au/industry/gambling/licences/bookmaker-licences-and-permits/bookmaker-licence-fees); [ABLIS – NT Sports Bookmaker Licence](https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/nt/sports%20bookmakers%20licence/3382); [NT – Apply for sports bookmaker licence](https://nt.gov.au/industry/gambling/licences/bookmaker-licences-and-permits/apply-for-or-renew-sports-bookmaker-licence) (via search summaries; page blocked on direct fetch)
- Senet on PRA licences generally: "On-course bookmakers can also take phone and internet bets if they hold the appropriate additional authorisation." — [Senet guide](https://www.senetgroup.com/news-and-insights/diversifying-into-new-markets-a-guide-to-obtaining-an-online-bookmaker-licence-in-australia/9)
- Senet on PRA licence timing: "the licence approval process typically takes between three to six months" (consultant estimate). — [Senet – Licensing for Bookmakers](https://www.senetgroup.com/sectors/wagering)
- POCT neutralises much of the NT tax advantage for Victorian customers because it is levied where the customer is, not where the licence is. — [VGCCC Wagering and betting (search summary)](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/wagering-and-betting)

### Inferences
- Why small operators pick Victoria: no state application fee, individual (not corporate) registration permitted, a mutual guarantee fund instead of a $200k-$250k bank guarantee, local regulator, and the ability to hybridise on-course and online. Why they pick NT: no PRA licence or VBA gatekeeping, no personal bookmaking-experience test, scalable corporate structure, 20-year term, and an established ecosystem of NT-licensed competitors and suppliers. The VBA's new Class I criteria ($1m net assets, corporate experience) suggest Victoria is deliberately keeping online entrants to experienced, well-capitalised individuals.
- Victoria's "present at the premises" rule means an online-only Victorian bookmaker must still operate from a specific approved Victorian premises with the bookmaker or an approved substitute (registered key employee) physically present during trading, which constrains 24/7 operation unless substitutes are rostered.

### Gaps
- No Victorian source gives a count of Class I (online-only) members or examples of recently approved online-only entrants.
- NT processing-time figures are consultant estimates; the NT Commission publishes none.

## Key Question 6: Partnership route: adding a new entrant to an existing bookmaker, buying/transferring a business or registration, nominees, and being a key employee while applying

### Takeaway
A VGCCC registration is personal and cannot be bought; what can be bought is the bookmaking business (client book, brand, stand/ballot position, systems), after which the buyer must obtain their own registration, VBA membership and licence. The VBA explicitly recognises entry "via acquisition" and a family class (Class F) for relatives of existing members who partner or incorporate with them. A new entrant can become a registered bookmaker's key employee (and approved substitute) first, which is the recognised way to accumulate the 50-100 race meetings of experience the VBA requires; but a partnership cannot be registered until each partner is independently a registered sole-trader bookmaker.

### Cited Findings
- Partnership prerequisite: "Before applying for a Bookmaking partnership, you must first ensure that all partners are approved sole trader bookmakers." — [VGCCC Bookmaker registration](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-registration)
- Partnership kit lodgement list: "An executed Deed of Partnership; National Police Certificate for each partner; Credit Report for each partner; Historical Personal Name extract from ASIC for each partner; Schedules A to L for each partner; Obtain and attach the Victorian Bookmakers Association Bond guarantee; A copy of the business plan including financial projections for the first 3 years ...; Consent for release of information by Law Enforcement Agencies form for each partner; Financial Information Release form for each partner"; the form records each partner's "% of Ownership – Voting / Income" and requires "written confirmation from the Victorian Bookmakers' Association Ltd of the default guarantee status of the proposed members of the partnership or of other suitable alternative arrangements". — [Partnership kit WAB011224](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB011224_Bookmaker-registration-partnership_190225-%281%29.pdf)
- Acquisition recognised by the VBA: "nowadays, with bookmaking businesses being tradeable assets, new Members are able to enter the profession via acquisition. In such circumstances, the VBA Board would determine the applicable Class of the business proposed to be acquired (or joined via partnership / incorporation) by the new Member." — [VBA Joining Criteria note 9](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Joining-Criteria-Fee-Framework-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)
- Change-of-control rules: a member must give the VBA at least 7 days' written notice before selling 10%+ of shares or changing structure "including by entry into a partnership, association, joint venture or incorporation"; the VBA may investigate the incoming person and veto the transaction. — [VBA Eligibility Policy cl 6](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Membership-Eligibility-Policy-and-Terms-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)
- Family route: Class F requires the applicant to be an individual, "Related to an Existing Member" (spouse/domestic partner, parent, sibling, child, grandparent, grandchild, aunt/uncle, first cousin, niece/nephew, step/adoptive relations), "currently in (or intending to form) a partnership, association, joint venture, or corporation ... with that Related Existing Member", 18+, 50 race meetings' experience, and no net asset test if the VBA is satisfied with the relative's finances; joining fee $750 with a 50% discount. — [VBA Eligibility Policy cl 2, 12](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Membership-Eligibility-Policy-and-Terms-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf); [VBA Joining Criteria](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Joining-Criteria-Fee-Framework-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)
- Nominee route for a company: a corporation applies for registration and simultaneously nominates an individual as nominee; "If an existing nominee resigns, is dismissed or ceases to manage or control the business, the registration holder must nominate another individual"; replacement must be lodged within 60 days. — [VGCCC FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf); [VGCCC Information sheet](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Gambling-information-sheet-Information-for-a-registered-bookmaker-(2).pdf)
- Key employee as a stepping stone: a registered key employee can be approved by the racing body as a substitute to "carry on the business of the registered bookmaker during a period of absence" (s 91AB); the VBA counts experience "as a Bookmaker's Key Employee and/or a Bookmaker's clerk" toward its 50/100 race-meeting thresholds with exposure to "operating keyboard / ledger, cash management ..., customer dispute resolution, assisting the managing bookmaker of race day operations, and liability management / hedging". — [Racing Act s 91AB](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/723edbc0-2ae6-3f51-bf06-3e51623bda11_58-6353aa177%20authorised.pdf); [VBA Joining Criteria note 1](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Joining-Criteria-Fee-Framework-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)
- Key employee registration: individual applies to VGCCC (kit WAB041224), 18+, no relevant offence in 10 years, NPC within 3 months, up to 10-year term; ABLIS notes the applicant must "consent to a national police check, finger and palm printing". — [VGCCC Bookmaker key employee](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-key-employee); [ABLIS – Bookmaker's Key Employee VIC](https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/vic/registration-as-a-bookmakers-key-employee/23919)
- The sole trader bookmaker form asks whether family members or key employees will be used (Q14) and whether the applicant is "associated with any other gambling licence/registration holder" (Q2), so a concurrent key-employee registration is contemplated. — [Sole trader kit](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB051224_Bookmaker-Registration-Sole-Trader_260225.pdf)

### Inferences
- The practical "partner with an existing bookmaker" route is: (1) get registered as that bookmaker's key employee; (2) be approved by Racing Victoria as their substitute and accumulate meetings; (3) apply for own VGCCC sole-trader registration; (4) join the VBA (Class F if related, otherwise the relevant class); (5) apply with the existing bookmaker for VGCCC partnership approval and a Racing Victoria partnership licence, or incorporate with them. Steps 1-2 can overlap a day job because key employees work race days.
- A nominee arrangement (company registered, experienced person as nominee, new entrant as associate/shareholder) is a legitimate way for a less-experienced investor to participate, but the associate still undergoes full VGCCC probity and the VBA may veto a 10%+ shareholder.

### Gaps
- No source addresses whether the VGCCC permits a person to hold a key employee registration and a bookmaker registration simultaneously (the FAQ says bookmakers may do key-employee duties but cannot be another bookmaker's substitute).
- No published data on going prices or recent sales of Victorian bookmaking businesses.

## Key Question 7: Realistic timeframes from cold start to first bet; keeping a day job; employment restrictions while registered

### Takeaway
No regulator publishes an end-to-end time. Assembling the official minimums gives a floor of roughly 3-4 months for someone who already meets the VBA experience test (8+ weeks VGCCC, 21 business days' VBA notice before trading, plus racing-body licensing that Senet puts at 3-6 months, partly overlapping), and more realistically 6-12 months once PFIAs, off-course premises approval, systems and AML programs are added. For a true cold start without bookmaking experience, the VBA's 100-race-meeting (or 3-year corporate) requirement adds one to several seasons of race-day work beforehand. Nothing found prohibits keeping other employment; the only employment-related rules are the misconduct-termination declaration and the bar on employing persons with relevant offences.

### Cited Findings
- VGCCC: "Each application is different" (no stated duration); generic "lodge ... at least eight weeks in advance"; incomplete applications "will be returned". — [VGCCC FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf); [VGCCC Application fees](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/resources-hub/application-fees)
- Document freshness constraints that shape sequencing: NPC and credit report must be under 3 months old at lodgement; photo within 3 months; VBA cash must have been held 6 months in the applicant's name. — [Sole trader kit](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB051224_Bookmaker-Registration-Sole-Trader_260225.pdf); [VBA Joining Criteria note 4](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Joining-Criteria-Fee-Framework-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)
- VBA: policy documents and business plan due "at least twenty-one (21) business days before commencing its operations"; VBA review costs "are to be borne by the Applicant"; VBA may change its policy on 14 days' notice. — [VBA Eligibility Policy cl 10.1(c), 10.5, 4.4](https://vicbookmakers.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/VBA-Membership-Eligibility-Policy-and-Terms-Effective-24-June-2025.pdf)

- Statutory presence requirement (bookmaker or approved substitute must be present at the approved premises when a bet is made) is the practical constraint on combining a day job with trading. — [Racing Act s 4(3)](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/723edbc0-2ae6-3f51-bf06-3e51623bda11_58-6353aa177%20authorised.pdf)

### Inferences (estimates, clearly labelled)
- Scenario A, experienced applicant (meets VBA Class I or on-course class criteria, funds seasoned 6+ months): preparation of documents 4-8 weeks; VGCCC determination 2-4 months (8-week floor plus bond gating); VBA interview/approval running in parallel; Racing Victoria licence and off-course premises approval 2-4 months after VGCCC (Senet's 3-6 months partly overlaps); PFIAs and launch readiness 1-3 months. Total: about 6-9 months; 4 months is the optimistic floor. This is my estimate from the sourced components; no regulator or practitioner publishes an end-to-end figure.
- Scenario B, cold start with no industry experience: add 1-3 racing seasons working race days as a clerk/key employee (100 meetings at, say, 1-2 meetings a week is 12-24 months), or 3 years inside a corporate bookmaker for Class I. Total: roughly 2-4 years to first bet unless the VBA Board exercises discretion or the applicant enters via family class or acquisition.
- Keeping a day job is feasible through the application stage (nothing prohibits it, and key-employee/clerk work is race-day work), but once trading the presence requirement and the Class I monthly deposit-ratio certification make a full-time commitment, or a rostered approved substitute, necessary. Public-sector employers (universities, councils) would need to be checked against their own secondary-employment/conflict policies; no gambling-law restriction was found.

### Gaps
- No practitioner or industry article quantifying actual elapsed time for a recent Victorian bookmaker registration was found; searches for case studies of new Victorian bookmakers in 2024-2025 returned nothing relevant.
- No rule on secondary employment or public-sector employment for registered bookmakers was located in gambling legislation; this would be governed by the employer, not the VGCCC.

## Key Question 8: Current VGCCC fee schedule and 2024-2026 changes affecting bookmaker registration

### Takeaway
As of the VGCCC's 1 July 2026 fee page there is still no bookmaker registration fee line (individual registration is fee-free per ABLIS). - The regulatory direction in Victoria is toward tighter conduct supervision of online bookmakers (NCPF, inducements, self-exclusion) rather than higher entry fees; entry is gated by the VBA's capital and experience tests, which were raised in 2025.

## Consolidated step list for a layperson (derived from the cited findings above)

1. Self-assess against VBA class criteria (experience, age, net assets, 6-month seasoned cash). If short on experience, get registered as a bookmaker's key employee (VGCCC kit WAB041224) and work race meetings for an existing bookmaker; seek substitute approval from Racing Victoria.
2. Assemble VGCCC documents: Victoria Police NPC (national name check), credit report, ASIC historical extract, passport photo, 3 years' tax returns and NOAs, audited/certified financials, financial institution letter, 3-year business plan with governance/compliance framework, Responsible Gambling Code (VBA code by default), family/associate disclosures.
3. Apply to the VBA in parallel (application form, statutory declaration of ownership, employment letters, police check, credit report, business plan, Board interview); obtain written confirmation of guarantee status for the VGCCC.
4. Lodge VGCCC sole trader/corporation application via the Portal (nominee and associate forms with it). Wait: no published time; plan 2-4 months. Decision published within 14 days.
5. On registration: pay VBA joining fee ($750-$5,000 ex-GST) and guarantee deposits; Class I members also lodge the $50,000 customer deposit surety and have website T&Cs approved.
6. Apply to Racing Victoria (and HRV/GRV if desired) for a club bookmaker's licence (13-step form; financial statement; off-course premises form; levy), and for approval of an approved off-course premises for telephone/internet betting under Racing Act ss 4B-4C. Licence year runs 1 Aug-31 Jul.
7. For sports: confirm events on the VGCCC approved list; sign Product Fee and Integrity Agreements with each Sports Controlling Body; obtain race-fields approvals from interstate racing bodies for interstate racing.
8. Build compliance: NCPF measures, AML/CTF program (AUSTRAC), self-exclusion (BetStop) integration, responsible gambling code, advertising rules; deliver policy documents to the VBA at least 21 business days before launch.
9. Trade only with the bookmaker or an approved substitute present at the approved premises; file VBA deposit-ratio certificates monthly for the first year; notify VGCCC of changes within 14 days; renew VGCCC registration at 10 years and VBA/racing licences annually.
