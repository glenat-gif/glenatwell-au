# BetMakers (ASX: BET) and "bookmaker-in-a-box" platforms for small Australian bookmakers (as at October 2026)

Scope note: Research conducted 9 October 2026. Sources are weighted toward BetMakers' own ASX announcements (FY25 results 28 Aug 2025; 1H FY26 results 27 Feb 2026; FY26 results 31 Aug 2026; Tabcorp Scheme Implementation Deed 10 Aug 2026) and Australian trade press (The Straight, SBC News, iGaming Business, Gambling.com). Anything older than 2024 is flagged as such. BetMakers does not publish a rate card for small bookmakers; where commercial terms are given below they are either (a) from a disclosed large-operator contract or (b) inference.

## Q1. What does BetMakers offer small Australian bookmakers, and what does the bookmaker get?

### Takeaway
BetMakers' current product for bookmakers is the cloud-based "Apollo" wagering platform (fixed odds + tote, pricing/trading, risk, account management, payments, compliance controls, white-label front end) plus optional 24/7 Managed Trading Services, racing content/data (RaceOdds, RaceLab) and tote access. It is marketed as "turnkey", launchable "in weeks" and "pre-approved by all major licensing bodies". KYC, responsible-gambling tooling, customer service and payments-provider relationships are not explicitly described as modules on the public site, and nothing public says BetMakers administers race-field/product fees or POCT on the bookmaker's behalf.

### Cited Findings
- BetMakers' public "Platforms" page describes Apollo as a full-stack, Australia-focused sportsbook covering fixed-odds and tote betting, with pricing engines, bet processing, risk tools, account management and payments, compliance controls and a customisable (white-label) front end "tailored to your brand"; it says Apollo is "Pre-approved by all major licensing bodies", is cloud-based with "continuous updates and minimal maintenance", and can be launched "in weeks". Related products listed: RaceBook+ (embedded/standalone white-label racebook, "fully managed"), GTX Digital (tote + fixed odds), BetLine retail terminals, RaceLab ratings, and Managed Trading Services (24/7 pricing, trading and risk management). Dedicated KYC, responsible gambling and customer-service modules are *not* described on the page. — [BetMakers Platforms page](https://www.betmakers.com/solutions/platforms)
- The corporate homepage groups the offer into Fixed Odds Services (pricing, data, front-end tools, risk management), Tote (Quantum, GTX, RaceBook+), Platforms & Terminals and Data & Informatics; it does not use the labels "Apollo", "Global Betting Services", "OM Apps" or "white-label apps" on the homepage. — [betmakers.com](https://www.betmakers.com/)
- BetMakers' financial reporting still segments revenue as "Global Tote" and "Global Betting Services" (GBS): FY26 Global Tote $49.3m (down 2.3%), Global Betting Services $43.3m (up 25.5%). — [Gambling.com, FY26 results](https://www.gambling.com/news/betmakers-posts-205-ebitda-jump-in-fy26-as-tabcorp-takeover-advances)
- In the 2022 NTD (Betr) contract, the services BetMakers contracted to supply were: "launch services, including developing the B2B Platform, providing platform services, product services, development services, managed trading services, data and data warehouse services, analytics services and support services." BetMakers retains all IP in the platform and any improvements. — [ASX announcement 21 Apr 2022](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20220421/pdf/45863ql6nftknw.pdf)
- For the 2025 CrownBet (Betfair Australia) deal, BetMakers supplies "its full wagering stack, including a fully customised deployment of the Apollo wagering platform, trading and risk management, content engine, and core platform technology". Apollo is described as handling fixed odds and tote with "customer account management tools, trading and risk services, compliance controls and a flexible frontend for racing-focused sportsbooks." — [SBC News, Dec 2025](https://sbcnews.co.uk/sportsbook/2025/12/04/betfair-australia-crownbet/); [European Gaming](https://europeangaming.eu/portal/latest-news/2025/12/08/197932/betmakers-signs-multi-year-agreement-to-launch-crownbet/)
- (Older, 2020) BetMakers' on-course bookmaker programme (Rob Waterhouse, Mark Sampieri) supplied "a new technology platform and automated products, including trading and pricing tools" and was "open to any on-course bookmaker moving online", with three commercial options: fixed-fee, turnover-based, or profit-share. — [InterGame, Apr 2020](https://www.intergameonline.com/igaming/news/australian-rails-bookmakers-sign-with-betmakers)
- (Older) BetMakers' "Waterhouse trading desk" is a B2B Managed Trading Services product where operators are "assigned an outsourced Waterhouse team, powered by BetMakers technology, to manage day-to-day bookmaking across more than 250,000 races per year worldwide including pricing and trading services and minimum guaranteed returns." — [Smallcaps](https://smallcaps.com.au/betmakers-signs-wagering-technology-deals-waterhouse-group)
- Trade commentary (2025) groups Punterstech, OpenBet, BetMakers, BetCloud and Generation Web as "end-to-end solutions for the smaller and/or fledgling bookmaker, lowering their barriers to entry". — [The Straight, Sep 2025](https://thestraight.com.au/two-more-brands-launch-on-punterstech/)
- Review-site listing of BetMakers-powered Australian brands (unverified consumer site; treat as indicative): BallrBet, BaggyBet, BetAus, BetDash, BetEstate, BetGold, Betit, BetLegends, BetLocal, BetSupreme, BetYouCan, BetZooka, BossBet, Chasebet, CrownBet, DiamondBet, EarlyCrow, FatBet, KnuckleBet, Marantelli Bet, Multis.com.au, Next2Go, OKEBET, PicnicBet, PlayWest, PonyBet, PremiumBet, Punt123, PuntX, ReadyBet, RealBookie, RobWaterhouse.com, StableBet, Swiftbet, TerryBet, UPCoz, UpYaGo, WishBet. The same site notes that sites on the same provider "often share the same odds and promotions". — [GoBet.com.au software providers FAQ](https://www.gobet.com.au/betting-sites/faq/which-software-providers-do-australian-betting-sites-use/)

### Inferences
- The 2022 Betr contract scope (platform + managed trading + data + support) is the best public template for what a "full-service" BetMakers deal contains. For a small bookmaker it would be the same modules at smaller scale: Apollo front end/app, trading via Managed Trading Services, account/payment rails, and compliance controls. The bookmaker almost certainly still has to supply: its own licence/registration and bond, its AUSTRAC enrolment and AML/CTF program, its race-field/product-fee registrations with each controlling body, POCT registrations in each state, payment merchant facilities in its own name (not confirmed either way), marketing, and customer-service staffing unless separately contracted.
- "Pre-approved by all major licensing bodies" likely refers to Apollo having passed the NT and state "approved betting system" certifications (see Q5), which is the main technical gating item for a Victorian registered bookmaker going online.

### Gaps
- No public BetMakers document itemises what a small-bookmaker package includes vs excludes (KYC vendor, payments, RG tools, customer support, product-fee administration). Would need a direct enquiry or a current client.
- Could not confirm whether the former Global Betting Services "white-label apps" (ex-OM Apps/Sportech-era assets) are still sold separately from Apollo.
- Could not locate any live bookmaker site footer saying "powered by BetMakers" (search returned none).

## Q2. Commercial terms (revenue share, set-up, minimums, who pays product fees/POCT)

### Takeaway
No public source discloses BetMakers' percentage take from a small bookmaker, and the "~60% cut" figure given to the user could not be verified anywhere. The only disclosed BetMakers revenue-share is the 2022 Betr contract: 25% of net gaming revenue stepping down 1 point a year, on top of a $2m establishment fee and $7.5m/yr minimum service fee, with caps. BetMakers' current stated model is a "hybrid of fixed fees and revenue share". BetMakers' older on-course offer gave three options (fixed fee, turnover-based, profit-share).

### Cited Findings
- 2022 NTD/Betr contract terms (ASX, 21 Apr 2022; the largest and only fully disclosed BetMakers platform contract): Platform Establishment Fee $2m; Launch Development Fee $500,000/month until go-live; Development & Service Fee minimum $7.5m p.a. from go-live ("minimum base fee of $75 million over the 10-year contract (before CPI increases)"); "Annual Fee based on a revenue sharing arrangement starting at 25% of ongoing net gaming revenue, reducing by 1% on each anniversary of the go live date during the 10-year contract as the business scales, less the annual Development & Service Fee"; annual fee cap $20m initially growing to $43m by year 10; BetMakers' max possible revenue ~$313m over 10 years; minimum ~$80m. BetMakers was the "exclusive technology and services provider in Australia and New Zealand" to the venture. — [ASX announcement 21 Apr 2022](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20220421/pdf/45863ql6nftknw.pdf); summarised at [Market Index](https://www.marketindex.com.au/news/lucrative-aust-based-partnership-for-perennial-loss-maker-betmakers-global) and [iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/sports-betting/news-corp-tekkorp-and-tgw-to-launch-betmakers-powered-wagering-venture/)
- 2022 contract term/termination: 10-year initial term from go-live, customer may extend 3 + 2 years at its sole discretion; BetMakers may terminate immediately for unremedied material breach (30 days' notice); customer may terminate if it ceases business, if go-live does not occur within 18 months of signing, or (after a corrective action plan process) for customary BetMakers default events; BetMakers retains all IP. — [ASX announcement 21 Apr 2022](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20220421/pdf/45863ql6nftknw.pdf)
- When Betr was sold to BlueBet (April 2024), Betr paid BetMakers staged exit fees: $3.75m paid March 2024, $2.25m due June 2024, up to $2m by September 2024 (dependent on migration timing), plus $500,000 per month if customer migration ran past 1 October 2024; Betr moved to a "reduced service arrangement" until migration. — [SBC News, Apr 2024](https://sbcnews.co.uk/sportsbook/2024/04/11/betmakers-acquires-racelab-assets-and-amends-betr-deal/); [NEXT.io](https://next.io/news/investment/betmakers-acquires-racelab-cuts-betr-ties/)
- CrownBet (Dec 2025, 5-year exclusive): "Commercial terms include a hybrid of fixed fees and revenue share, designed to expand recurring revenue and align BetMakers with the long-term success of CrownBet." No percentages disclosed. — [SBC News](https://sbcnews.co.uk/sportsbook/2025/12/04/betfair-australia-crownbet/); [AGB](https://agbrief.com/news/australia/04/12/2025/betmakers-enters-exclusive-5-year-agreement-to-launch-crownbet/)
- (Older, 2020) On-course bookmaker programme: "three commercial options: fixed-fee, turnover-based and profit-share". — [InterGame, Apr 2020](https://www.intergameonline.com/igaming/news/australian-rails-bookmakers-sign-with-betmakers)
- (Older, ~2022) BetMakers' revenue was described as "directly linked to that of its customers, as it holds revenue share agreements with around 30 different wagering platforms", and as coming partly from "fees based on a percentage of bets placed using its technology, as well as through managed services and data distribution". — [SBC News, 2022](https://sbcnews.co.uk/retail/2022/10/28/betmakers-closes-q1-fy23-with-aus64m-cash-balance-as-global-operations-ramp-up/); [Dealroom profile](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/betmakers_technology_group)
- Competitor Punterstech says it "publishes operational costs upfront in its onboarding packs" but gives no figures publicly; its CEO describes revenue share as "the model BetMakers pioneered at scale". — [The Straight, "After the gold rush"](https://thestraight.com.au/after-the-gold-rush-punterstech-sees-opportunity-in-a-wagering-industry-in-flux/)
- Generic (non-Australian, vendor blog, low reliability) white-label sportsbook benchmarks quote provider take of "10–15% of GGR or a defined percentage of NGR". — [search result summary; blog source unverified](https://blask.com/blog/?p=3791)

### Inferences
- The "60% cut" the user was quoted is not contradicted by anything public, but it is far above the 25%-of-NGR headline in the Betr deal. Two reconciliations are plausible: (a) small operators with no fixed minimum fee pay a much higher percentage of *net revenue after product fees, POCT and payment costs* (i.e., a profit-share, which was one of the three 2020 options), where 50-60% of residual profit to the provider is consistent with the provider carrying trading, hosting and support cost; or (b) the quote bundles managed trading plus platform. Either way, the base (turnover vs GGR vs NGR vs net profit after fees) matters more than the headline percentage, and the user should obtain the written definition of "net revenue".
- The Betr contract shows BetMakers' preferred structure for serious clients is fixed minimums plus revenue share, 10-year term, exclusivity, and provider retention of all IP. A small bookmaker should expect shorter terms but the same IP and exclusivity posture.
- Product fees and POCT are statutory obligations of the licensed bookmaker; nothing public suggests BetMakers assumes them. Expect them to be deducted before any "net" split.

### Gaps
- No public rate card, set-up fee, monthly minimum, or revenue-share percentage for small Australian bookmakers on BetMakers, BetCloud, Punterstech or Generation Web. BetMakers' annual reports do not break out small-operator pricing.
- Could not verify the origin or definition of the "60%" figure.

## Q3. Which small Australian bookmakers run on BetMakers, how many partners, churn/closures, and BetMakers' financial health / counterparty risk

### Takeaway
BetMakers reports partner momentum (eight new Apollo customers live in Q2 FY26, eight more contracted) but no total partner count. After a loss-making FY24 and the loss of its biggest Australian platform client (Betr) in 2024, it restructured, migrated Australian platform customers to Apollo, and is now modestly EBITDA-positive (FY26 revenue $92.6m, adjusted EBITDA $14.1m, net loss $5.2m, no debt). The decisive counterparty event is the binding agreement (10 Aug 2026) for Tabcorp to acquire 100% of BetMakers at $0.24/share (~$267m), targeted to complete in Tabcorp's 3Q FY27 (roughly Jan-Mar 2027) subject to shareholder, court, ACCC and gaming-regulator approvals. A new entrant would therefore be contracting with a vendor about to be owned by Australia's largest incumbent wagering operator.

### Inferences
- Solvency risk at BetMakers is now low (no debt, positive EBITDA and operating cash flow, and a funded acquirer). The counterparty risk has changed shape: from "will the vendor survive" to "will the vendor, as a Tabcorp subsidiary, keep serving tiny third-party bookmakers that compete with TAB, on what terms, and with what priority?" Tabcorp's stated synergy case is about replacing its own tech, not about growing the small-bookmaker channel. Regulators' consent conditions may also impose ring-fencing, but nothing is public yet.
- Churn evidence shows BetMakers' platform clients can and do leave (Betr), but with substantial exit fees negotiated in that large contract; a small bookmaker should expect termination/migration fees in its own contract.
- "Eight new Apollo customers in a quarter" alongside only ~3 new Australian bookmaker launches per year (trade press) suggests many Apollo go-lives are migrations of existing partners or international clients, not new Australian start-ups.

### Gaps
- BetMakers does not publish the number of Australian platform/bookmaker partners, nor a churn figure; the annual report was not reviewed in full (only results announcements).
- FY26 year-end cash balance not in the results announcement (would be in Appendix 4E / annual report).
- No public statement from Tabcorp or BetMakers about the future of third-party small-bookmaker contracts post-acquisition.

## Q4. Alternatives for a Victorian-registered individual with modest capital, and comparative economics

### Takeaway
The realistic "bookmaker-in-a-box" vendors in Australia are Punterstech (18 brands, the most active launcher in 2024-25), Generation Web (~26 brands per consumer listings), BetCloud (Melbourne-based, ~12 brands) and BetMakers; OpenBet is cited as a competitor but serves larger operators. None publish pricing. Market context is unfavourable: small-bookmaker turnover reportedly halved in a year, ~106 online bookmakers existed in Sept 2024, and a string of small brands closed in 2024-26. Larger platforms named in the brief (Kambi, Amelco, PointsBet B2B) are not evidenced as serving Australian micro-bookmakers.

- Victoria: on-course bookmaker licensees "must use an 'approved betting system' that meets certification and testing requirements from an independent third-party tester"; software suppliers to bookmakers generally do not need their own licence but their software must be approved. NT corporate bookmakers must use a regulator-approved system with ISO/IEC 27001 annual audits. — [Senet Group, wagering technology service providers](https://www.senetgroup.com/sectors/wagering-technology-service-providers)

### Inferences
- For a Victorian-registered individual with modest capital, the realistic options are the four domestic white-label vendors above; all require the bookmaker to hold its own registration, bond and off-course permit, so the platform choice does not change the licensing burden. Punterstech appears to be the most actively marketed to new entrants; BetCloud has a demonstrated VGCCC-licensed client (TempleBet) and is Melbourne-based; BetMakers' recent public wins are large (CrownBet, Stake, Racing Victoria) and its pending Tabcorp ownership makes it the least certain option for a micro-operator.
- Because providers run shared odds across their brands ("sites on the same provider often share the same odds and promotions"), a small white-label brand's differentiation is mostly marketing and customer relationships, not pricing.
- Economics comparison cannot be made on published numbers; the only known structural options are fixed fee, turnover-based or profit-share (BetMakers 2020), and "hybrid fixed + revenue share" (BetMakers 2025).

### Gaps
- No public pricing for Punterstech, BetCloud or Generation Web.
- Could not verify whether Kambi, Amelco, OpenBet, Sportech or PointsBet offer anything to Australian micro-bookmakers; no evidence found that they do.
- Racing Victoria's off-course permit criteria document could not be fetched (404).

## Q5. Who is legally the bookmaker, what liability stays with them, and regulator enforcement 2023-2026

### Takeaway
In a white-label set-up the registered individual (or their corporate licensee) is the bookmaker: the customer's contract is with the licensee's own published terms, the licence bond backs customer claims, and regulators penalise the licensee for breaches even when a third-party platform or vendor caused them. No regulator decision was found that held a platform provider liable instead of the bookmaker.

### Cited Findings
- "Legal and regulatory obligations are not diminished where an on-course bookmaker or NT sports bookmaker utilises an outsourced service"; parties should contractually agree "the respective responsibilities of the parties"; suppliers generally do not need their own licence. — [Senet Group](https://www.senetgroup.com/sectors/wagering-technology-service-providers)
- NT: all licensed sports bookmakers must "promulgate a detailed set of terms and conditions for wagering which both parties are bound by when an account is opened and each time a wager is struck" i.e. the customer contracts with the bookmaker brand, not the platform. — [NTRC decision G v BetEasy](https://dth.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0004/900499/G-v-BetEasy.pdf)
- Sportsbet (NTRWC, decision 19 June 2025): penalty 500 penalty units (A$92,500) for marketing SMS sent to self-excluded customers; the error arose on "a third-party marketing platform" operated by new staff; the Commission treated it as the licensee's breach. — [iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/marketing-affiliates/northern-territory-penalises-sportsbet-marketing-breach/)
- Betr fined A$20,655 for contacting a self-excluded gambler; its defence that staff were using a database being updated with Commission data was rejected. — [The Citizen (syndicated)](https://www.thecitizen.co.tz/tanzania/business/australian-bookmaker-penalized-for-targeting-self-excluded-gambler-4169558)
- BoomBet (small NT-licensed operator) fined ~A$18,000 and ordered to refund a $1,667 deposit in early 2025 for insufficient responsible gambling procedures (July-Sept 2023). — [The Straight](https://thestraight.com.au/boom-bust-wagering-firm-announces-departure-from-online-wagering-space/)
- Sportsbet penalised A$313,000 (Nov 2025) for failing to send activity statements to at least 6,161 customers. — [search summary; iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/?p=383778)
- BudgetBet (WA) collapse: licence bond is being used by the regulator to meet customer claims after the operator surrendered the licence. — [The Straight](https://thestraight.com.au/wa-regulators-left-to-sift-through-fallout-after-online-bookie-budgetbets-clo/)
- NT Racing and Wagering Act 2024 (in force from 1 July 2025) replaced the Racing and Betting Act 1983; the regulator is now the NT Racing and Wagering Commission; it can suspend or cancel licences for offences such as unapproved affiliate arrangements. — [NTRWC Annual Report 2024-25](https://dth.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0006/1571784/ntrw-commission-annual-report-2024-25.pdf); [Vixio](https://vixio.com/insights/gc-northern-territory-moves-against-illegal-affiliate-activity)
- AUSTRAC/AML: "a purely procedural approach to compliance is not sufficient"; March 2026 AML/CTF reforms emphasise risk-based identification and management of ML/TF risk. — [Senet Group](https://www.senetgroup.com/sectors/wagering-technology-service-providers)
- (Historic, 2012) the NT Racing Commission investigated Betezy Pty Ltd including "all past and existing White Label agreements entered into by Betezy with third parties", showing white-label arrangements attract regulator scrutiny of the licensee. — [NTRC Betezy decision](https://dth.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0004/232897/1207_betezy_pty_ltd.pdf)
- In the 2022 BetMakers/Betr deal the venture (not BetMakers) was to "apply for a sports bookmaker licence under which it will operate"; obtaining licences was the customer's obligation and a condition of go-live. — [ASX 21 Apr 2022](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20220421/pdf/45863ql6nftknw.pdf)

### Inferences
- A Victorian registered bookmaker on a white-label platform remains the reporting entity for AUSTRAC, the licensee for VGCCC/Racing Victoria, the party liable for POCT in each state and product fees to each controlling body, and the respondent to customer complaints; the platform's failures (odds errors, outages, mis-sent marketing, RG tooling gaps) are the bookmaker's regulatory problem unless recovered contractually.
- Customer funds: regulators rely on the licensee's bond; whether the platform or the bookmaker holds the merchant/payment account is contract-specific and was not publicly documented for any vendor.

### Gaps
- No VGCCC or NTRWC enforcement action 2023-2026 was found that specifically names a white-label platform provider or allocates responsibility between provider and bookmaker.
- No public evidence on who holds the merchant facility and client-funds account under BetMakers or competitor white-label contracts.

## Q6. Practical dependency risks (outages, odds quality, data portability, exclusivity, termination)

### Takeaway
Public contract evidence (Betr 2022) shows BetMakers deals are exclusive, long-term, with provider-retained IP and negotiated exit fees; the Betr-to-BlueBet migration shows customer data can be migrated off, but only after paying for it. Shared odds across white-label brands and the vendor's own cost-cutting (GBS restructuring, legacy platform shutdown) are the main operational dependencies; the pending Tabcorp ownership adds strategic-priority risk.

### Cited Findings
- Exclusivity and term: BetMakers was "exclusive technology and services provider" to Betr for 10 years from go-live; the CrownBet deal is a "five-year exclusive agreement" with BetMakers as "sole technology provider". — [ASX 21 Apr 2022](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20220421/pdf/45863ql6nftknw.pdf); [AGB](https://agbrief.com/news/australia/04/12/2025/betmakers-enters-exclusive-5-year-agreement-to-launch-crownbet/)
- IP: "BetMakers shall retain all intellectual property rights in the existing and future improvements to the BetMakers software, and in any materials created... including the B2B Platform." — [ASX 21 Apr 2022](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20220421/pdf/45863ql6nftknw.pdf)
- Portability precedent: Betr's customer base was migrated to BlueBet's platform in 2024 under an amended agreement with staged fees and a $500k/month penalty for late migration; betM's existing customer base was migrated *onto* Apollo in six weeks in 2025. — [SBC News](https://sbcnews.co.uk/sportsbook/2024/04/11/betmakers-acquires-racelab-assets-and-amends-betr-deal/); [European Gaming](https://europeangaming.eu/portal/latest-news/2025/04/09/180063/mgb-completes-migration-of-betm-to-betmakers-apollo-platform/)
- Termination mechanics in the 2022 deal: 30-day cure for material breach; customer exit if go-live misses 18 months; corrective-action-plan process before customer can terminate for provider default. — [ASX 21 Apr 2022](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20220421/pdf/45863ql6nftknw.pdf)
- Odds/trading commonality: brands on the same provider "often share the same odds and promotions". — [GoBet.com.au](https://www.gobet.com.au/betting-sites/faq/which-software-providers-do-australian-betting-sites-use/)
- Vendor cost-cutting: BetMakers restructured the GBS staff cost base and migrated all Australian platform customers to Apollo in FY25 to cut cloud/infrastructure costs; operating expenses fell from $65.3m (FY24) to $49.4m (FY26). — [SBC News 1H FY25](https://sbcnews.co.uk/retail/2025/02/27/betmakers-1h-fy25-report/); [FY26 results](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260831/pdf/073gwvjh3gwwx1.pdf)
- Tabcorp intends to replace its own platforms with BetMakers' and to extract $30m of cost synergies; regulatory consents from gaming/racing authorities are a condition. — [Tabcorp ASX 10 Aug 2026](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260810/pdf/072l3kbq40167l.pdf)

### Inferences
- A small bookmaker should expect: exclusivity for the term; no ownership of platform IP; a contractual right to its customer list (standard, but confirm in writing) and a priced migration/termination path; a notice period (not disclosed publicly for small clients); and shared pricing with other brands on the same platform, which limits ability to differentiate on odds.
- Operational risk of outage or odds-feed error sits with the bookmaker vis-a-vis the regulator and customers; contractual SLAs and indemnities are the only recourse. No public outage history for Apollo was found.
- After the Tabcorp acquisition, the small-bookmaker channel could be de-prioritised, re-priced, or ring-fenced by regulatory condition; a new entrant signing in late 2026 is signing during this uncertainty.

### Gaps
- No published SLAs, uptime history, or notice periods for small-bookmaker contracts at any vendor.
- No public dispute or litigation between BetMakers and a small bookmaker was found.
