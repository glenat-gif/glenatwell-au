# Personal, structural, financial and practical considerations for a first-time Victorian bookmaker (as at 9 October 2026)

Scope note: Victoria, Australia. "Hard requirement" = statute/regulator/employer policy text. "Practitioner view" = quoted operator, trade press or vendor. Anything I could not verify is listed under Gaps. Dates are given wherever a source carries one.

## Key Question 1: Employment while applying — Deakin, local council, VPS Code, and VGCCC disclosure

### Takeaway
Neither Deakin's current Declaration of Interest Procedure (effective 17 Dec 2025) nor the Local Government Act 2020 contains a specific ban on staff holding a bookmaker registration, but both treat paid external work and business/financial interests as disclosable, Deakin reserves the right to decline to approve a conflict "within the University's risk appetite", and most Victorian councils require CEO/manager approval of any secondary employment. The VGCCC application itself forces disclosure of all income sources over $30,000 for three years and three years of tax returns, so current employment will be visible to the regulator; I found no Victorian rule that bars an "associate" with a public-sector job.

### Cited Findings

**Deakin University (hard requirement)**
- Deakin's current Declaration of Interest Procedure "is effective from 17 December 2025" and "applies to all University members and associates and includes the controlled entities"; "members" include academic and professional staff (cl. 1, 3, 34(7)). — [Deakin Policy Library, Declaration of Interest Procedure](https://policy.deakin.edu.au/view.current.php?id=00019)
- A "Disclosable Interest" is "an interest that may impact upon an individual's duties and responsibilities to the University" and expressly covers "voluntary or paid activities, financial interests, company director roles" (cl. 11); external employment is defined as work for another person or organisation for payment or other consideration, "including being a director" (cl. 34(3)). — [Deakin Declaration of Interest Procedure](https://policy.deakin.edu.au/view.current.php?id=00019)
- Declaration must be made "prior to any COI arising" or "as soon as practicable" after awareness (cl. 13), before engagement and "not less than annually" thereafter (cl. 15(1)); the relevant leader considers the declaration (cl. 16), General Counsel must independently review and endorse declarations and management plans (cl. 19), and assessment is "subject to determination by the Vice-Chancellor (or delegate)" (cl. 24). — [Deakin Declaration of Interest Procedure](https://policy.deakin.edu.au/view.current.php?id=00019)
- The University "may decline to approve a COI" that cannot be managed "within the University's risk appetite" (cl. 8); a perceived conflict exists where "it could reasonably be perceived, or give the appearance, that a competing interest could improperly influence" duties (cl. 6(3)); failure to declare can lead to disciplinary action under the Staff Discipline procedure (cl. 30). The procedure does not use the words "reputational risk" or "University values". — [Deakin Declaration of Interest Procedure](https://policy.deakin.edu.au/view.current.php?id=00019)
- Deakin's older (2014) Conflict of Interest Procedure required full-time staff wishing to engage in outside employment to "complete an Authority to Approve Outside Employment form and fully disclose, in writing, any potential conflict of interest", with approval from both the manager and a relevant senior officer; part-time and casual staff could do outside work without approval "provided there is no conflict of interest or interference with the staff member's University duties". The download copy is dated roughly 7.7 years old and appears superseded by the 2025 procedure, but an "Authority to Approve Outside Work" form is still listed in the Policy Library. — [Deakin Conflict of Interest Procedure (2014)](https://policy.deakin.edu.au/document/view-current.php?id=19&version=1); [Authority to Approve Outside Work form](https://policy.deakin.edu.au/download.php?id=427&version=1&associated=)
- Reputational context: Deakin employs Professor Samantha Thomas, a public-health researcher specialising in "the commercial and political determinants of health" and "the prevention and reduction of gambling related harm", whose research was cited by UK MPs calling for a gambling advertising ban (June 2020) and who has given evidence to Australian parliamentary inquiries on gambling. — [Deakin staff profile](https://www.deakin.edu.au/about-deakin/people/samantha-thomas); [Deakin research news](https://www.deakin.edu.au/research/research-news/articles/uk-mps-call-for-gambling-ad-ban,-drawing-on-deakin-research)

**Victorian local councils (hard requirement, varies by council)**
- Under the Local Government Act 2020, council staff are "relevant persons" for conflict-of-interest purposes when they sit on a delegated or community asset committee, provide advice to council or a committee, exercise a delegation, or perform a delegated function or statutory power; a relevant person with a conflict "must disclose the interest and not participate in the decision-making process on the matter". — [Local Government Victoria, "In the Public Interest" (Oct 2020)](https://www.localgovernment.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0025/173635/Conflict-of-interest-guide-FINAL-October-2020.pdf)
- Sections 127 and 128 of the Act require councils to keep a conflict of interest register for councillors and council staff. — [Boroondara conflict-of-interest registers page](https://boroondara.vic.gov.au/your-council/council-administration/council-documents/conflict-interest-registers-councillors-and-council-staff)
- The Act requires the CEO to implement a code of conduct for all staff, which must include "capacity for disciplinary action when employees are in breach of the Code", and all codes must be public. — [Maddocks, Local Government Act 2020 employment summary](https://www.maddocks.com.au/insights/new-local-government-act-2020-passed-what-does-this-mean-for-employment-at-councils)
- IBAC found council approaches differ: "some councils only require management approval where a conflict may exist, while other councils mandate approval for any secondary employment, regardless of whether or not a conflict has been identified"; IBAC recommends councils re-check approved secondary employment after promotion and keep approved and rejected applications centrally. — [IBAC, Managing corruption risks associated with conflicts of interest](https://www.ibac.vic.gov.au/media/1145/download)
- Examples: Loddon Shire allows secondary work only where it doesn't clash with the council role and only with CEO approval, via a secondary employment declaration kept on the personnel file; Wyndham requires approval from both manager and director before starting outside work, keeps a register of secondary-employment declarations; Greater Dandenong uses a "Conflict of Interest - Secondary Employment Disclosure Form"; Manningham (adopted 5 March 2026) requires disclosure within 5 days of identification, routed to the CEO through the manager. — [Loddon Staff Code of Conduct](https://www.loddon.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/v/3/our-council/plans-and-strategies/staff-code-of-conduct.pdf); [Wyndham Conflict of Interest Policy 2024](https://www.wyndham.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-08/Conflict%20of%20Interest%20-%20Compliance%2C%20Process%20%26%20Guidance%20Policy%20-%202024.pdf); [Greater Dandenong disclose-interest page](https://thesource.greaterdandenong.vic.gov.au/disclose-interest); [Manningham Conflicts of Interest Policy (Mar 2026)](https://www.manningham.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-03/Conflict%20of%20Interest%20Policy%20-%20EMT%20adopted%20-%205%20March%202026.PDF)
- Failure to disclose "can result in criminal prosecution in a court... and/or disciplinary action under the Code of Conduct (for Council staff)"; Wangaratta's policy requires the CEO to notify the Chief Municipal Inspector if a staff member fails to declare. — [Greater Dandenong guidance](https://thesource.greaterdandenong.vic.gov.au/_flysystem/filerepo/A7080203); [Wangaratta Conflict of Interest Policy 2023](https://www.wangaratta.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/conflict-of-interest-policy-2023.pdf)
- Reputational context: many Victorian councils maintain gambling-harm policies. Knox adopted a Gambling Harm Prevention Policy on 23 Feb 2026 covering "all forms of gambling harm" (Knox EGM losses $84m, up 6.05% on 2024-25); Maroondah adopted a Gambling Harm Prevention and Minimisation Policy on 15 June 2026 (EGM losses $64.2m in 2024-25); Whitehorse's paused 2025 draft would have extended scope "to online gambling and sports betting"; the MAV and 24 councils joined the Alliance for Gambling Reform. Conversely Warrnambool revoked its gaming policy as "ineffective". — [Knox policy](https://www.knox.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-03/Knox-Gambling-Harm-Prevention-Policy-V1.pdf); [Knox news](https://www.knox.vic.gov.au/whats-happening/news/84-million-lost-electronic-gaming-machines-knox); [Maroondah policy 2026](https://www.maroondah.vic.gov.au/files/assets/public/v/2/documents/about-council/policies/maroondah-city-council-gambling-harm-prevention-and-minimisation-policy-2026-final.pdf); [Whitehorse July 2025 update](https://yoursay.whitehorse.vic.gov.au/gambling); [MAV Alliance page](https://www.mav.asn.au/what-we-do/policy-advocacy/public-health-safety/gambling/alliance-for-gambling-reform); [Warrnambool report](https://www.westvicbrolga.com.au/p/warrnambool-council-scraps-gambling-policy)

**VPS Code of Conduct (applies to Victorian public service; councils have their own codes)**
- Clause 3.8 of the Code of Conduct for Victorian Public Sector Employees requires employees to "ensure that all other paid or volunteer employment activities do not conflict with their public sector role and obtain approval to engage in any other paid employment". — [VPSC summary of employee obligations](https://vpsc.vic.gov.au/?p=172607)
- The Department of Justice policy under clause 3.8 defines outside employment as "any employment or business in addition to their departmental employment" and treats an activity as a business "where goods and/or services are provided for which payments are made". — [DJCS outside employment policy](https://ftp.justice.vic.gov.au/node/1023)

**What the VGCCC application discloses about current employment (hard requirement)**
- Sole-trader applicants must supply "tax returns and Notices of Assessment for the past 3 financial years", a credit report (Equifax/illion/Experian), ASIC current and historical personal name extract, National Police Certificate dated within 3 months, and "audited and certified financial statements"; corporations also need an accountant's/auditor's statement, creditors listing and a financial institution letter. — [VGCCC Bookmaker registration](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-registration)
- The partnership application kit (WAB011224, Feb 2025) requires Schedules A–L for each partner; Schedule K lists "all sources of funds/financial benefits from any source (in excess of $30,000 in any one year) for the past (3) financial years", Schedule L lists directorships/secretary roles, and Q9 asks "Does any partner have any other associations within the gambling industry? In any jurisdiction and in any capacity." The Financial Information Release form authorises the VGCCC to obtain bank, accountant and adviser records "to be satisfied that the applicant and each of its associates is of sound and stable financial background". — [VGCCC Bookmaker registration – partnership kit (PDF)](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB011224_Bookmaker-registration-partnership_190225-%281%29.pdf)
- Providing false or misleading information is an offence with a fine of up to 60 penalty units; applicants "must notify the VGCCC in writing immediately" of any change in information between lodgement and decision. — [VGCCC Bookmaker registration](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-registration)
- "Associate" under the Gambling Regulation Act 2003 includes anyone with a relevant financial interest or power able to exercise "significant influence" over the business, and specifically "those with a partnership interest of 10 per cent, greater income or voting entitlements" and shareholders of 10%+; associate approval lasts only for the bookmaker's registration term and must be re-done at renewal. — [VGCCC Bookmakers FAQ (Mar 2025, PDF)](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)
- VGCCC decisions on registration "are also published on the VGCCC website within 14 days", and the fact a person has applied for or holds a registration (and any disciplinary particulars) may be released without consent. — [VGCCC Bookmakers FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)

### Inferences
- A Deakin staff member would need to declare the intended bookmaking business (as external employment/financial interest/directorship) before registering; because the procedure now routes declarations through General Counsel with VC-level determination, approval is not a formality and a gambling business sits close to the University's public-health research identity, so a management plan or refusal is plausible.
- For the council role, the applicant should assume secondary-employment approval by the CEO or manager/director is required regardless of whether a conflict exists, because IBAC reports many councils mandate it; if the council has an adopted gambling-harm policy (as Knox, Maroondah and others do), a perceived conflict is likely to be raised.
- Because the VGCCC publishes registration decisions and the application discloses all income sources, the applicant cannot realistically keep the registration invisible to either employer; non-disclosure to the employer would be the larger risk.

### Gaps
- I could not find any provision in Victorian bookmaker rules or VGCCC directions restricting "associates" who hold public-sector or university roles; absence of evidence, not confirmed absence.
- The applicant's specific council's code of conduct and secondary-employment policy were not identified (council not named); the approval route (CEO vs manager) varies by council.
- Deakin's 2025 procedure text was read via summary; whether the separate "Authority to Approve Outside Work" form is still mandatory for full-time staff under the 2025 procedure could not be confirmed.

## Key Question 2: Business structure, personal liability, tax, and advisers

### Takeaway
Victorian bookmaker registration attaches to a natural person or a corporation (which must appoint a personally liable nominee); partnerships are a VGCCC-approved trading arrangement between already-registered sole traders, not a separate licence. Tax is layered: GST on the gambling margin (Division 126), Victorian wagering and betting tax at 15% of net wagering revenue above $1m (monthly returns), payroll tax above $1m wages, and ordinary income tax; there is no published figure for adviser costs, but a specialist gambling-law practice exists.

### Cited Findings

**Who holds the registration (hard requirement)**
- "An individual aged 18 years or more, or a corporation may apply to the VGCCC for registration as a bookmaker"; a corporation "must nominate a person to be approved by the VGCCC to be responsible as the nominee", which "ensures one person is accountable for the management and control of the bookmaking business, with the person nominated and approved to be liable under the Act as a registered bookmaker"; the nominee "could be prosecuted for any breaches that occur". — [VGCCC Bookmakers FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)
- Application types are sole trader, corporation or partnership; "before applying for a Bookmaking partnership, you must first ensure that all partners are approved sole trader bookmakers". Registration lasts 10 years. — [VGCCC Bookmaker registration](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-registration)
- Trusts are contemplated: where the applicant or associated entity is a trust, all directors of a corporate trustee and unit holders/beneficiaries with power to remove the trustee are associates requiring approval. — [VGCCC Bookmakers FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)
- A separate Club Bookmaker's Licence from Racing Victoria, Harness Racing Victoria or Greyhound Racing Victoria is also required; "You must not undertake any duties as a bookmaker without having both your registration and licence approved". Licences allow fielding at race meetings and "in some cases, to conduct 24-hour trading at racecourses, approved off-course premises or sports grounds". — [VGCCC Bookmakers FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf); [VGCCC Bookmaker registration](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-registration)
- Government fees: a 2010 Regulatory Impact Statement records that "since the early 1990s there have been no Government fees associated with applications for bookmaking related registrations" for natural persons and proposed full cost recovery for corporations (461.85 fee units, $5,520 in 2010); the VGCCC fee table effective 1 July 2024 does not list a bookmaker registration fee. — [Gambling Regulation Amendment (Bookmaking Related Registration Fees) RIS 2010](https://www.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2019-11/Gambling-Regulation-Amdnendment-Bookmaking-Related-Registration-Fees-Regulations-2010-RIS.pdf); [VGCCC application fees](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/node/3215936)

**Victorian-specific liability trap (practitioner/legal)**
- The Victorian Court of Appeal (reported 10 July) reversed a $150,000 betting debt owed to former Warrnambool bookmaker David McLauchlan because the bookmaker had not obtained a signed telephone-betting authorisation and lodged it with Racing Victoria, so the debt was unenforceable. — [Warrnambool Standard](https://www.standard.net.au/story/8692412/court-reverses-150k-betting-debt-in-landmark-victoria-case)

**GST (hard requirement)**
- "Gambling sales are taxable, but you only apply GST to the margin between money received and prizes paid out"; worked example: $86,000 bets less $20,000 prizes = $66,000 margin, GST $6,000 (1/11). Bad debts written off can be added to prizes paid; a negative margin carries forward against the next activity statement. — [ATO, GST when conducting gambling activities – calculating your margin](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/your-industry/gst-when-conducting-gambling-activities/calculating-your-margin); [ATO, accounting for gambling sales](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/your-industry/gst-when-conducting-gambling-activities/accounting-for-gambling-sales)
- The Federal Court describes Division 126's "global GST amount" as applying GST "to the margin of the person providing the gambling opportunity" because taxing each wager "would be impractical"; prizes paid to overseas gamblers are included in the calculation (International All Sports v FCT, 2011). — [Federal Court 2020 judgment](https://www.judgments.fedcourt.gov.au/judgments/Judgments/fca/single/2020/2020fca1295); [Public Accountant, amendments to MT ruling](https://publicaccountant.com.au/features/amendments-to-mt-ruling/)

**Victorian wagering and betting tax (hard requirement)**
- Rate 15% of net wagering revenue (including GST) from customers located in Victoria above a tax-free threshold of $1,000,000 per financial year; rate table currently shows the period 1 July 2024 to 30 June 2026 and no change from 1 July 2026 is posted. — [SRO current rates](https://sro.vic.gov.au/about-us/rates-and-statistics/current-rates/wagering-and-betting-tax-current-rates); [SRO FAQ](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/wagering-and-betting-tax-–-frequently-asked-questions)
- "You must register online before the end of the first month in which you become liable for the tax"; "Returns are lodged monthly and payments are due within 30 days of the end of each month", including nil months; free/bonus bets are included in revenue "even if the customer does not contribute any money"; odds boosts count as winnings; late lodgement attracts penalty tax and interest. — [SRO FAQ](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/wagering-and-betting-tax-–-frequently-asked-questions)
- Historical rates: 8% above $500k (2019), 10% above $1m (2023-24), 15% from 1 July 2024. — [SRO archive](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/wagering-and-betting-tax-archive)

**Payroll tax (hard requirement)**
- From 1 July 2025 the Victorian payroll tax-free threshold is $1,000,000 annually ($83,333 monthly); standard rate 4.85% per secondary sources. — [SRO changes to payroll tax threshold](https://sro.vic.gov.au/payroll-tax/changes-payroll-tax-threshold); [RosterElf guide](https://www.rosterelf.com/guides/employment-law/victoria-payroll-tax)

**Income tax**
- ATO ruling IT 2655 addresses whether a punter is carrying on a business of betting; the courts stress "systematic organisation" as the prerequisite for assessable gambling income. — [ATO IT 2655 (PDF)](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/pdf/pbr/it2655.pdf); [Revenue Law Journal 2013](https://www5.austlii.edu.au/au/journals/RevenueLawJl/2013/5.pdf)
- (Comparative) HMRC: "an organised activity to make profits out of the gambling public will normally amount to trading. An example of this is the bookmaker." — [HMRC BIM22018](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/business-income-manual/bim22018)

**Other state licence cost comparison (for context)**
- NT sports bookmaker licence: $29,000 application fee (2025-26) and a $200,000 bank guarantee; 5% tax on gross profit capped at $1.41m; a third-party guide estimates first-year outlay of ~A$300–350k dominated by the guarantee. — [ABLIS NT sports bookmaker licence](https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/nt/sports%20bookmakers%20licence/3382); [NT bookmaker licence fees](https://nt.gov.au/industry/gambling/bookmaker-licences-and-permits/bookmaker-licence-fees); [gamblingmaps NT operator guide](https://gamblingmaps.org/map/regulations/australia/northern-territory/for-operators)

**Advisers**
- Senet Legal operates a dedicated bookmakers sector practice. — [Senet Legal – Bookmakers](https://senetlegal.com/sectors/bookmakers)

### Inferences
- In Victoria the registered person remains personally on the hook: a sole trader is directly liable; a corporate structure still requires a personally liable nominee, and a partnership requires each partner to hold their own registration. A company or trust can hold the economic interest, but the probity, liability and bond obligations follow the individual(s).
- A small book with Victorian customers generating under $1m net wagering revenue would pay no Victorian wagering and betting tax but must still register and lodge monthly nil returns once liable; at the friend's quoted scale (A$2m/month turnover) the $1m NWR threshold would be exceeded within the year.
- Given GST on margin, monthly SRO returns, race-fields reconciliations and VGCCC financial monitoring, a bookkeeper plus an accountant familiar with Division 126 is a practical necessity from day one; costs are not published.

### Gaps
- No source states whether a Victorian partnership can include a corporate partner, nor the current VGCCC corporate registration fee in 2025-26 dollars.
- PAYG instalment treatment and typical accountant/lawyer fees for a small bookmaker were not found.
- Partnership Act 1958 (Vic) default rules (dissolution on death, joint liability) were not independently retrieved in this research.

## Key Question 3: Capital, cash flow and variance

### Takeaway
The regulator demands a VBA bond guarantee and three-year financial projections but publishes no capital minimum; the real constraints are the VBA bond (amount not public), platform set-up and revenue share, the product-fee stack (Racing Victoria's CEO says "total cost of product for a mid-tier operator is 55 per cent, 60 per cent" of revenue once POCT is included), and the liability swings illustrated by Betr's Anamoe exposure at "more than five times what we'd budgeted". The "A$800k/month at 7–10% margin, set and forget" picture is not supported by any practitioner account I found; the only small-operator data point is that smaller bookmakers' turnover fell about 50% in the 12 months to September 2024.

### Cited Findings
- Bond: "the VGCCC cannot determine a bookmaker's application until satisfied that the applicant has appropriate bond arrangements"; the guarantee "can be arranged between the applicant and the Victorian Bookmakers' Association in the determined amount" or by alternative arrangement approved by the VGCCC. — [VGCCC Bookmakers FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)
- Non-VBA applicants who have not separately organised a s94A guarantee "are not eligible to apply for a Club Bookmaker's Licence"; partnership applications need "written confirmation from the Victorian Bookmakers' Association Ltd of the default guarantee status of the proposed members". — [Racing Victoria club bookmaker application (via search)](https://racingvictoria.com.au/wagering/-/media/ab7b828f442f4c1ebf14b844c599c02c.ashx); [VGCCC partnership kit](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB011224_Bookmaker-registration-partnership_190225-%281%29.pdf)
- The partnership application asks for "Estimated gross turnover for the next financial year", intended race meetings, bet types, telephone usage and "Workload rosters", plus a business plan with "financial projections for the first 3 years... accepted methods of accepting gambling payments and proportions (cash etc), where and how the business is to operate". — [VGCCC partnership kit](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB011224_Bookmaker-registration-partnership_190225-%281%29.pdf)
- Product fees: Racing Victoria's new race-fields regime from 1 October 2024 moved to a monthly turnover-and-revenue calculation, removed fees on free-bet turnover, set "a specific 0.5 per cent rate for on-course bookmaker activity", and replaced threshold discounts with a graduated structure; CEO Aaron Morrison: "the total cost of product for a mid-tier operator is 55 per cent, 60 per cent"; smaller bookmakers "saw a roughly 50 per cent drop in turnover over the previous 12 months". — [The Straight, 13 Sep 2024](https://thestraight.com.au/racing-victoria-slashes-race-fields-fees-in-a-bid-to-revive-wagering)
- Racing Victoria FY24: wagering turnover down 10.2% to $7.9bn, with "stronger than average WSP gross margins" offsetting the decline; 2024 spring carnival wagering fell 8.2% to $2.3bn while bookmaker margins across the carnival were 15.9% (up from 15.7%). — [Racing Victoria FY24 results](https://www.racingvictoria.com.au/news/2024/11/annual-report-and-fy24-results); [The Straight, spring turnover](https://thestraight.com.au/no-spring-in-victorian-turnover-as-wagering-slump-drags-on/)
- Variance (practitioner): Betr CEO Andrew Menz on the 2022 Cox Plate: "Our liability on Anamoe was more than five times what we'd budgeted". — [Champion Bets on Betr](https://www.championbets.com.au/news-article/betr)
- Veteran Victorian bookmaker Anthony Doughty (45 years): "there are no certainties in horse racing and new bookies aren't replacing old"; on-course "was 90 per cent of our turnover. Now it's less than one per cent". — [The Straight, 2 May 2024](https://thestraight.com.au/?p=3748)
- Platform costs (vendor/consultant blogs, not Australian-specific): white-label set-up "typically cost between $10,000 and $40,000" with ongoing revenue share "typically 10-20%" or fixed monthly fees; another guide gives "$10,000–$50,000 + revenue share (typically 20–40%)"; a casino vendor warns revenue share "begins to cut into 10–30% of your Net Gaming Revenue (NGR) every single month". BetMakers (Tabcorp agreed to acquire it for A$267m EV, Aug 2026) supplies white-label platforms to Australian brands but publishes no pricing. — [track360 guide](https://track360.io/blog/turnkey-sportsbook-software-operator-guide); [sourcecodelab guide](https://sourcecodelab.co/white-label-sportsbook-vs-custom-build-operators-guide-2026/); [Symphony Solutions](https://symphony-solutions.com/insights/no-revshare-and-white-label-casino-costs); [mlex on Tabcorp/BetMakers](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/articles/2511375)
- BetStop cost recovery charges operators per check against the register, which Responsible Wagering Australia says "creates a disincentive for WSPs to undertake frequent checks". — [RWA BetStop review submission](https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/betstop-review-responsible-wagering-australia.pdf)
- Scale comparators: Betr H1 FY turnover $807.4m (six months), net win up 12.6% vs turnover up 25.2%; Betstar (sold 2014) turned over ~$300m/yr; Mad Bookie (sold 2017) ~$80m annualised turnover with 15,000+ customers. — [The Straight on Betr](https://thestraight.com.au/betrs-turnover-jump-whets-appetite-for-more-growth); [SmartCompany on Betstar](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/finance/alan-eskander-why-i-sold-betstar-for-25-million-to-ladbrokes/); [SBC News on Mad Bookie](https://sbcnews.co.uk/oceania/2017/04/13/topbetta-australia-ups-racing-profile-mad-bookie-acquisition/)

### Inferences
- Arithmetic on the applicant's figures: A$800k/month turnover at 7–10% gross margin is A$56k–80k/month gross win (A$670k–960k/yr). Taking Morrison's 55–60% "cost of product" for a mid-tier operator as an upper bound for race-fields fees plus POCT, then GST at 1/11 of margin, platform revenue share of 10–30% of NGR, BetStop/KYC per-check costs, payments and bonus costs, net operating profit before owner drawings could plausibly be 20–35% of gross win, i.e. roughly A$130k–340k/yr — an inference from the cited cost drivers, not a sourced figure. The applicant's own margin assumption is also above what Betr's net-win vs turnover trend implies for a growing book, though below spring-carnival margins of ~16%.
- "Set and forget" is contradicted by every operator-side account found (liability blow-outs, 50% turnover swings, compulsory monthly returns, trading control).
- Because the VBA guarantee is the regulator's primary consumer-protection mechanism and VGCCC monitors the applicant's "financial resources" on an ongoing basis, personal capital must be demonstrable and cannot be fully quarantined behind a company.

### Gaps
- The VBA guarantee-fund contribution and membership fee were not found publicly (VBA has no retrievable website in search); the Racing Victoria club bookmaker application and wagering rules PDFs returned 404 on fetch.

## Key Question 4: Banking and payments

### Takeaway
I found no documented case of a licensed Australian bookmaker being de-banked; the Council of Financial Regulators' de-banking work (2022) covered fintechs, crypto exchanges and remitters, not wagering. The only wagering-adjacent evidence is anecdotal: Victorian on-course bookies reporting EFTPOS modems "shut down by providers, who may not want to be linked to gambling". Payments are shaped by the 11 June 2024 credit-card ban, with PayID/PayTo offered by specialist providers such as Monoova.

### Cited Findings
- Government terms of reference (21 March 2022) asked the CFR for advice on de-banking "for financial technology firms, digital currency exchanges, and remittance providers"; the CFR's August 2022 recommendations include 30 days' notice, documented reasons and access to IDR. — [Treasury de-banking page](https://treasury.gov.au/review/de-banking); [CFR report](https://www.cfr.gov.au/publications/policy-statements-and-other-reports/2022/potential-policy-responses-to-de-banking-in-australia/pdf/potential-policy-responses-to-de-banking-in-australia.pdf)
- AUSTRAC: "indiscriminate and widespread closure of accounts across entire financial services sectors should be discouraged"; banks are expected to make risk-based decisions. — [AUSTRAC de-banking statement](https://www.austrac.gov.au/news-and-media/media-release/austrac-statement-2021-de-banking); [AUSTRAC guidance](https://austrac.gov.au/node/998)
- Supreme Court of Victoria (2026) upheld Westpac's contractual right to close a gold dealer's accounts as "high risk". — [Banking Day](https://www.bankingday.com/court-upholds-debanking-of-gold-dealer-by-westpac-anz)
- Victorian on-course anecdote (May 2024): cashless/tap-and-go is seen as helpful by some bookies, "but some have had their modems shut down by providers, who may not want to be linked to gambling." — [The Straight, 2 May 2024](https://thestraight.com.au/?p=3748)
- Credit card ban: from 11 June 2024 licensed online wagering providers cannot accept credit cards, credit-linked digital wallets or cryptocurrency; it applies to online/telephone betting "including those provided by on-course bookmakers"; ACMA enforces with fines up to $247,500; a review is due after June 2026. Less than 5% of deposits came via credit at the time. — [Infrastructure Dept credit card ban](https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/media-communications-arts/gambling/credit-card-ban); [ACMA credit ban](https://www.acma.gov.au/credit-ban); [Vixio](https://www.vixio.com/insights/gc-australia-impose-ban-credit-cards-online-gambling)
- Monoova markets a wagering product with PayID instant funding, card/wallet deposits, fraud checks, deposit-limit enforcement and PayTo for both funding and payouts. — [Monoova wagering](https://monoova.com/wagering); [Monoova 2025 trends](https://www.monoova.com/blog/wagering-payments-trends-2025)
- NSW is reforming the "Customer Debit Funds Trust Accounts" framework for licensed betting service providers (segregated customer funds). — [Liquor & Gaming NSW industry alert](https://www.liquorandgaming.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/1217543/industry-alert-conduct-of-licensed-betting-service-providers.pdf)
- Third-party account use: Australian bookmakers have been warned about third-party accounts because syndicates use them to evade restrictions. — [The Straight](https://thestraight.com.au/australian-bookies-warned-about-third-party-accounts/)

### Inferences
- Chargeback risk is structurally lower after the credit-card ban (PayID/Osko are irrevocable bank transfers), but AML/CTF onboarding and bank risk appetite still apply; a new sole-trader bookmaker with a public-sector day job should expect enhanced due diligence from banks and payment providers.

### Gaps
- No bank-by-bank policy on serving small licensed bookmakers was found; no Victorian customer-funds segregation requirement equivalent to NSW's was identified.
- Whether Zepto or Azupay serve wagering operators could not be confirmed.

## Key Question 5: Partnering with an established bookmaker — mechanics, succession, disputes, the three-way split

### Takeaway
A Victorian bookmaking partnership is a VGCCC-approved trading arrangement in which every partner is already a registered sole-trader bookmaker; the regulator requires an executed deed of partnership, VBA confirmation of each member's guarantee status, and full probity on each partner, and can suspend the partnership approval. Nothing in the regulator's material addresses what happens on a senior partner's death or retirement beyond the corporate-nominee replacement rule, so the deed carries that risk. The friend's "two sites, A$2m/month, split three ways" implies gross win of roughly A$140–200k/month before the cost stack, which at the inferred 20–35% net margin would be in the order of A$110k–280k per partner per year.

### Cited Findings
- "If you are a registered bookmaker and wish to enter into a partnership with another bookmaker, you must seek approval from the VGCCC to operate in a trading partnership with another registered bookmaker. All partners must be registered bookmakers." Approval is by the "Approval to be a member of a trading bookmaking partnership" form. — [VGCCC Bookmakers FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)
- VCAT review is available for refusal of "an application for approval of registered bookmakers to be in partnership" and for "a decision to suspend an approval of registered bookmakers to be in partnership". — [VGCCC Bookmakers FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)
- Partnership kit requirements: "An executed Deed of Partnership", NPC, credit report, ASIC historical extract, Schedules A–L and law-enforcement/financial release forms "for each partner", VBA bond guarantee, and a business plan including "experience in managing a business". — [VGCCC partnership kit](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/WAB011224_Bookmaker-registration-partnership_190225-%281%29.pdf)
- Partners with a 10%+ interest are "associates" requiring their own approval, re-done at each renewal. — [VGCCC Bookmakers FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf)
- Key employees: a "bookmaker's key employee" is someone employed "in a managerial capacity, authorised to make decisions relevant to the business operations"; a key employee may act as a bookmaker's substitute only with Racing Victoria/HRV/GRV approval; registration valid up to 10 years, Victoria only. — [VGCCC bookmaker key employee](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-key-employee); [ABLIS key employee registration](https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/vic/registration-as-a-bookmakers-key-employee/23919)
- Corporate succession rule: "If an existing nominee resigns, is dismissed or ceases to manage or control the business, the registration holder must nominate another individual to be approved as a nominee"; the VGCCC must be told within 14 days. — [VGCCC Bookmakers FAQ](https://vgccc.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-03/Frequently-asked-questions-Bookmakers-%282%29.pdf); [VGCCC Bookmaker registration](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-registration)
- Family-bookmaker precedents: Marantellibet is "family run... started by Gavin Marantelli who started operating as a track bookmaker in the early 70s before going online... in 2022". TopSport (co-owner Lloyd Merlehan; CEO son Tristan Merlehan) sold its customer database, brand and IP to BlueBet/Betr in 2025, with Tristan joining Betr as chief trading officer. — [horsebetting.com.au](https://horsebetting.com.au/online-bookmakers/); [SBC News, 5 Feb 2025](https://sbcnews.co.uk/featurednews/2025/02/05/bluebet-topsport-au-15m/)
- Succession sentiment: Doughty, asked whether his children should join the trade: "Of course not, there's no future in it"; "No applications for on-course bookmaker licences have been made in four or five years" (his estimate). — [The Straight, 2 May 2024](https://thestraight.com.au/?p=3748)
- Fiduciary duty between partners: in Birtchnell v Equity Trustees (HCA 1929, a Victorian partnership), a partner who took an undisclosed profit share from a transaction within the partnership's business was liable to account to the other partners, and his executors remained liable after his death. — [High Court judgment (PDF)](https://www.hcourt.gov.au/sites/default/files/eresources/1929/HCA/24.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because each partner must hold their own registration and VBA guarantee, the new entrant cannot "ride" on the mentor's licence; conversely, the mentor's clean 40-year regulatory history and a signed deed help the business-plan and "experience in managing a business" limbs of the application, and prior registration as a key employee would evidence that experience.
- On the senior partner's death or retirement, the regulator's partnership approval is tied to the named registered bookmakers; the surviving partner would need a fresh VGCCC approval (or a sole-trader continuation) and the deed must settle goodwill, client list, telephone-authorisation records, platform contract and bond obligations — none of which the regulator's material addresses.
- Three-way split arithmetic (inference): A$2m/month turnover at 7–10% margin is A$140–200k/month gross win (A$1.68–2.4m/yr). Victorian NWR at that scale exceeds the $1m threshold, so 15% POCT applies to the excess, plus race-fields fees, GST on margin and platform share; if net is 20–35% of gross win, A$335k–840k/yr is available for three partners, i.e. roughly A$110k–280k each before tax and before any capital/working-capital servicing. Treat as a sensitivity range, not a forecast.
- Typical dispute triggers (inference from the fiduciary case and the regulator's forms): trading control (who sets prices and limits), undisclosed side-books or gambling-industry associations (Q9 of the kit), allocation of losses against unequal capital, and one partner's probity event suspending the whole partnership approval.

### Gaps
- No reported Victorian bookmaking-partnership dispute was found; the Partnership Act 1958 (Vic) default rules were not retrieved.
- No source on whether a Victorian bookmaker's client list or telephone-authorisation records can be assigned to a successor.
- The friend's arrangement (two sites, A$2m/month, three-way split) is unverified; the partner-income range above depends entirely on the margin and cost assumptions stated.

## Key Question 6: Lifestyle and operational realities

### Takeaway
Operator accounts and regulatory records show a trade that is physically demanding on-course ("Siberia" at Flemington), commercially squeezed (about 20 full-time Victorian bookmakers left, per Doughty), and compliance-heavy online: minimum-bet-limit obligations with 21 published exclusions, pre-verification of customers, monthly activity statements, BetStop checks, and an NT private member's bill (Oct 2026) proposing a positive duty of care and $10m penalties. Australian regulators currently hold there is no general duty of care to problem gamblers, but a Federal Court unconscionable-conduct claim (Gassner) was on foot and its outcome is unknown.

### Cited Findings
- Doughty (The Straight, 2 May 2024): "only about 20 full-time bookmakers left working in Victoria"; some bookies work "about ten race days a year, mostly as a hobby"; Flemington is "an unhealthy environment" with wind, glare and rain, another rails bookie calls it "Siberia"; "The Casino was the beginning of the end"; customers want "multis, free bets and sports"; "As a profession, it's just about finished". — [The Straight](https://thestraight.com.au/?p=3748)
- Racing Victoria Minimum Bet Limit (from 1 Oct 2016): approved off-course WSPs must accept fixed-odds bets to win $2,000 (metro) / $1,000 (non-metro), place $800/$400, on Victorian thoroughbred races after final acceptances; Victorian on-course bookmakers are excluded because they already have minimum-bet rules in Schedule 7 of the Rules of Racing; 21 exclusions include "systematic multiple identical (or similar) bets from related/connected parties or from the same IP address", suspected non-beneficial ownership, proxy servers, scraping, bonus bets and integrity concerns. — [Racing Victoria MBL FAQ](https://racingvictoria.com.au/wagering/minimum-bet-policy-faq)
- HRV's MBL policy (1 July 2024) requires $2,000/$800 (Group 1), $1,000/$400 (metro), $500/$200 (country). — [HRV Minimum Bet Liability Policy](https://www.thetrots.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/RW-PO-003-Minimum-Bet-Liability-Policy.pdf)
- Bonus abuse enforcement example: NT Racing Commission upheld CrownBet's closure of an account flagged by rule-based monitoring for profiting from a bonus bet, voiding bets and returning the $500 deposit. — [G v Crownbet (NTRC)](https://dth.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0011/718805/G-v-Crownbet.pdf)
- DSS defines bonus abuse as using bonuses "in a way other than intended to increase or guarantee their chances of winning" and minimum betting limits as the minimum "a bookmaker must accept without restricting or closing a customer's account". — [DSS betting restrictions report](https://www.dss.gov.au/system/files/resources/final_report_-_betting_restrictions_report.pdf)
- NCPF obligations: identity verification at registration and before betting (replacing the 72-hour rule), monthly activity statements to active customers, and BetStop (live 21 Aug 2023) requiring closure of accounts of self-excluded people for 3 months to lifetime. — [DSS gambling reforms](https://dss.gov.au/communities-and-vulnerable-people-programs-services-gambling/gambling-reforms); [BetStop page](https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/media-communications/media-gambling-laws-regulation/gambling/betstop-national-self-exclusion-register)
- Victorian registered bookmakers must adopt and comply with a Responsible Gambling Code of Conduct and wear an ID card on-course. — [VGCCC Bookmaker registration](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-registration)
- Duty of care: NT Racing Commission (Luxbet v S) — "the bookmaker owes no such duty, and the onus remains on the client"; Centrebet v P — "no general duty of care exists except where exceptional circumstances prevail", relying on Reynolds v Katoomba RSL (NSWCA 2001). — [Luxbet v S](https://dth.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0010/364843/Luxbet-v-S.pdf); [Centrebet v P](https://dth.nt.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0006/261393/140428-Centrebet-v-P.pdf)
- Gassner v Sportsbet and Beteasy (Federal Court, filed ~June 2023): claim for ~$606,000 in losses alleging "unconscionable conduct" and that operators "enticed and encouraged him to gamble"; mediation was listed for 27 October; no outcome located. — [AAP](https://aapnews.com.au/news/gambler-sues-online-bookies-over-his-600-000-loss)
- NT bill (8 Oct 2026, independent MLA Justine Davis): deposit caps of $500/month (18–21) and $1,000/month (22–24), an outright ban on bonus bets and VIP schemes, "a 'positive duty' requiring bookmakers to take all reasonable steps to prevent customer harm", maximum penalty raised from $194,000 to $10 million or 10% of revenue; applies to ~50 NT-licensed online bookmakers; Attorney-General: "you can't legislate personal responsibility". — [ABC News, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-10-08/online-gambling-regulation-nt-bill-inducements-deposit-caps/107229354)
- Regulatory/discipline exposure: Doughty himself was suspended 4 months and fined $25,000 (2015) after taking ~3,374 bets totalling ~$911,000 in breach of his Club Bookmaker Licence conditions and record-keeping rules. — [Racing Victoria RAD Board decision](https://www.racingvictoria.com.au/integrity/victorian-racing-tribunal/~/media/81e96c94225146df97a7bdbbb7aad477.ashx); [Just Horse Racing](https://www.justhorseracing.com.au/general-news/snippets/victorian-bookie-charged-for-taking-improper-bets/307736/amp)
- Customer-side friction: a Victorian bettor's withdrawal was blocked for extra ID checks and the account closed for "suspicious activity", prompting complaints to VGCCC, Racing Victoria and the VBA. — [Whirlpool thread](https://forums.whirlpool.net.au/archive/9pxj208z)

### Inferences
- A small online book must staff or automate seven-day coverage of racing and evening sports, KYC, MBL compliance, BetStop checks, activity statements and dispute handling; the MBL exclusions give the operator lawful grounds to restrict multi-accounting/arbers, but each restriction is a potential complaint to Racing Victoria or the VGCCC.
- The direction of travel (NT positive duty, inducement bans, higher penalties; Victorian councils expanding harm policies to online betting) increases personal reputational and compliance risk for a named sole-trader/nominee over a 10-year registration.

### Gaps
- No first-person account from a small Australian online bookmaker about hours, mental load or customer-service volume was found; Doughty's account is on-course only.
- Racing Victoria's wagering rules PDF (levy, record-keeping, disputes) returned 404 and was not read.

## Key Question 7: Exit and optionality

### Takeaway
Small Australian books do sell, but as asset sales of the customer database, brand and IP rather than the licence: TopSport went to Betr for A$15m guaranteed (2025) and Mad Bookie for the greater of A$400k or 24x average monthly net gaming revenue (2017); Betstar fetched A$20–25m on ~A$300m turnover (2014). The licence itself is personal and non-transferable, and the main buyers are consolidators who migrate customers and shut the brand.

### Cited Findings
- TopSport/BlueBet (announced 5 Feb 2025, completed ~1 Apr 2025): asset sale of "customer database, brand and intellectual property, material contracts... and selected employees"; upfront A$10m (70% cash/30% scrip) with earn-out guaranteed to at least A$5m, total guaranteed A$15m; funded by a A$15m placement; customers migrated to the Betr platform before completion with "all anticipated cost synergies... realized within just 55 days"; 63 million rows of customer data migrated. — [SBC News, 5 Feb 2025](https://sbcnews.co.uk/featurednews/2025/02/05/bluebet-topsport-au-15m/); [SBC News, 1 Apr 2025](https://sbcnews.co.uk/sportsbook/2025/04/01/bluebet-topsport-takeover/); [ASX announcement](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20250205/pdf/06f6cgybqqjk6k.pdf)
- Mad Bookie/TopBetta (Apr 2017): Melbourne-based, 15,000+ customers, ~A$80m annualised turnover; price "the greater of AUD$400,000 (less specified expenses), or 24 times Mad Bookie's average monthly net gaming revenue over the 12 months following completion"; TopBetta CEO: "a ready-made, active database makes a lot of sense in an industry that has high customer acquisition costs". — [SBC News, 13 Apr 2017](https://sbcnews.co.uk/oceania/2017/04/13/topbetta-australia-ups-racing-profile-mad-bookie-acquisition/); [vLex](https://au.vlex.com/vid/topbetta-holdings-ltd-asx-677143633)
- Betstar/Ladbrokes (Apr 2014): reported A$20–25m; ~A$300m annual turnover; Alan Eskander: small and medium bookmakers were finding it increasingly hard to compete, "Offshore bookmakers are coming into Australia and investing enormous amounts of money." — [SmartCompany](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/finance/alan-eskander-why-i-sold-betstar-for-25-million-to-ladbrokes/)
- Consolidation trail: Mad Bookie, ClassicBet, BestBet, TopBetta were later folded into PlayUp; NextBet acquired CrossBet (late 2025) and reportedly PlayUp (2026); Entain shut Betstar and Bookmaker.com.au and merged accounts into Ladbrokes; Star Sports bought dormant Vinbet's IP and predicted "further consolidation at the smaller end of the market". — [The Straight, who owns racing's customers](https://thestraight.com.au/?p=14542); [BeforeYouBet NextBet/PlayUp](https://www.beforeyoubet.com.au/nextbet-acquires-playup-set-launch-march-1st); [BeforeYouBet Ladbrokes](https://www.beforeyoubet.com.au/news?page=4)
- Platform lock-in (vendor advice): "negotiate player-database portability upfront". — [sourcecodelab guide](https://sourcecodelab.co/white-label-sportsbook-vs-custom-build-operators-guide-2026/)
- Registration is personal, 10 years, renewable, and the only transferable things in these deals were data/brand/IP. — [VGCCC Bookmaker registration](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-gambling-providers/bookmakers/bookmaker-registration)

### Inferences
- The Mad Bookie formula (24x monthly NGR = 2x annual NGR, floor A$400k) and TopSport's structure (upfront plus earn-out tied to retained customers) suggest a small book's exit value is a low single-digit multiple of annual net gaming revenue, payable largely on customer retention, and that the brand is usually extinguished.
- Database value depends on clean, verified, consented customer data and a platform contract that allows export; a white-label contract with revenue share and no portability clause can materially reduce exit value.

### Gaps
- No 2025–26 sale of a Victorian-registered (as opposed to NT-licensed) small bookmaker was found.

## Key Question 8: Insurance

### Takeaway
No Australian source describes an insurance program specifically for licensed bookmakers; specialist brokers overseas identify cyber liability and technology E&O as the core lines for betting operators, insurers are hardening cyber terms (100–200% rate rises for weak controls), and the Australian Bookmakers Network lists an insurance partner (iSure). Cost figures are not available.

### Cited Findings
- JKJ (specialist sports-betting/esports broker): "the common insurance challenges for organizations in the sports betting and gaming space are Cyber Liability and Technology Errors & Omissions"; "in many cases, insurance is dictated by a contract". — [JKJ sports betting & esports](https://jkj.com/our-services/commercial-insurance/esports-gaming/)

### Inferences
