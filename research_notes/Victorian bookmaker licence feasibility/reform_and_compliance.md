# Australian and Victorian online wagering regulatory reform and compliance obligations for a small online bookmaker (status as at 9 October 2026)

Scope note: Victoria is treated as the home jurisdiction. Where a date is given as "enacted" it means the instrument has passed and (unless stated) commenced; "proposed/pending" means not yet law or not yet commenced. Secondary (press / law-firm) sources are identified as such; several primary pages (ACMA, APH, SRO, VGCCC) blocked automated fetching, so some items rest on reputable secondary reporting and are flagged.

## Key Question 1 — Federal layer: Interactive Gambling Act 2001 (IGA), ACMA, the National Consumer Protection Framework (NCPF), credit/crypto ban, Murphy inquiry, the 2026 Gambling Reform Act, and national-regulator / licensing proposals

### Takeaway
The federal layer has moved from "pending" to "enacted" during 2026: the Interactive Gambling Amendment (Gambling Reform) Act 2026 received Royal Assent on 26 August 2026 (per a third-party tracker; official Act text not retrieved) and most measures commence 1 January 2027. It is an advertising-restriction, inducement-restriction and cost-recovery-levy package, not the total ad ban or national regulator the Murphy inquiry recommended; the government's 12 May 2026 response "noted" rather than accepted the 31 recommendations and expressly rejected a federal regulator. The ten NCPF measures (BetStop, pre-verification at sign-up, activity statements, deposit limits, staff training, inducement limits, consistent messaging) are all in force and are where ACMA is actually fining small operators.

### Cited Findings

**IGA / ACMA baseline obligations and enforcement**
- ACMA fined Tabcorp A$158,400 (reported Feb 2026) for 426 online in-play bets across 32 tennis matches between Feb 2024 and Jun 2025; Tabcorp blamed a third-party provider but ACMA still penalised it because of a prior 2021 breach, and Tabcorp entered an enforceable undertaking to review tennis bet-closing processes. ACMA Commissioner Carolyn Lidgerwood: operators that rely on third-party providers to close betting "cannot outsource their legal responsibilities." — [The Straight](https://thestraight.com.au/tabcorp-fined-again-after-offering-in-play-tennis-betting); [Mediaweek](https://www.mediaweek.com.au/tabcorp-pays-158400-fine-for-illegal-sports-bets/)
- Earlier Tabcorp in-play fine of A$262,920 for 854 in-play bets across 69 tennis matches (Apr–Oct 2023), attributed by Tabcorp to a technical bug. — [iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/legal-compliance/tabcorp-fine-in-play-betting-acma-blocking)
- Entain (Ladbrokes/Neds) fined approx. A$13,320 — described as the maximum ACMA could issue for that particular IGA breach — for in-play bets on a LIV Golf event after the wrong start time was entered; ACMA said Entain lacked internal procedures to prevent or detect the error. — [GGB News](https://ggbnews.com/article/acma-fines-entain-for-offering-online-in-play-bets)
- Senet (25 Mar 2026): ACMA's enforcement focus is on domestic compliance — safer gambling, spam messaging (VIP/BetStop-integration spam has been penalised) and trade-promotion lottery models; since August 2023 ACMA has used court-enforceable undertakings "which can require costly process reviews." — [Senet 2026 Gambling Outlook](https://www.senetgroup.com/news-and-insights/aml/2026-gambling-outlook)

**NCPF measures (all enacted)**
- The NCPF has 10 measures applying to all licensed online wagering providers; the government's response to ACMA's review reported measures implemented including voluntary pre-commitment deposit limits, restrictions on betting inducements, a ban on providing credit (limited on-course telephone exemption), and staff training to the national Unit of Competency CHCFIN005. — [DSS Gambling reforms](https://dss.gov.au/communities-and-vulnerable-people-programs-services-gambling/gambling-reforms); [Government response to ACMA credit betting report](https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/government-response-to-the-acma-credit-betting-prohibitions-report.pdf); [Tas Treasury NCPF page](https://www.treasury.tas.gov.au/liquor-and-gaming/gambling/community-information/national-consumer-protection-framework)
- Consistent gambling messaging ("Chances are you're about to lose" etc.) and wagering staff training took effect 30 March 2023; BetStop (the National Self-Exclusion Register, operated by IXUP Ltd and regulated by ACMA) launched 21 August 2023. Providers cannot open an account or accept a bet from, or market to, a registered person and must promote BetStop on websites, apps and marketing. — [DSS media release](https://ministers.dss.gov.au/media-releases/12236); [Infrastructure Dept](https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/department/media/news/betstop-launch-21-august)
- Mandatory customer pre-verification (verify identity at registration and before any bet) replaced the 72-hour window; government said it was expected to be in place by end-September 2023. — [Minister Rowland media release](https://minister.infrastructure.gov.au/rowland/media-release/betstop-be-introduced-august)
- Victoria's VGCCC states that from 14 November 2022 all online bookmakers with Victorian customers must send monthly activity statements, and that Victorian bookmakers/WSPs must adhere to all NCPF measures. — [VGCCC (via search summary of bookmaker licensing page)](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/gambling/wagering-and-sports-betting/about-wagering-and-sports-betting/responsible-gambling-code-conduct)

**Murphy inquiry and the government response**

- PM Albanese declined the recommendation to move oversight to a federal body; the Bill "does not create a federal regulator" and the NT Racing and Wagering Commission retains de-facto national licensing role. — [IMGL/Addisons](https://www.imgl.org/publications/imgl-magazine-volume-3-no-1/future-gambling-regulation-in-australia/); [Blask commentary](https://blask.com/newsroom/australia-online-betting-regulation-ntrwc-reform/)

**Interactive Gambling Amendment (Gambling Reform) Act 2026 — enacted, commencing mainly 1 Jan 2027**
- Timeline: exposure draft 29 May 2026; Bill introduced 2 July 2026 and referred to Senate Environment and Communications Legislation Committee (report 17 Aug 2026); Government–Opposition amendment package agreed; passed House 18 Aug 2026, Senate 19 Aug 2026. — [Senet, 19 Aug 2026](https://www.senetgroup.com/news-and-insights/australias-gambling-advertising-reforms); [Hall & Wilcox, 3 Jul 2026](https://hallandwilcox.com.au/news/proposed-wagering-advertising-reforms-new-restrictions-for-sport-media-and-online-platforms-in-australia/); [Infrastructure Dept exposure draft](https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/exposure-draft-interactive-gambling-amendment-gambling-reform-bill-2026.pdf)

- Advertising (new IGA Part 7C): live-sport blackout — as introduced, 5 min before to 5 min after (6am–8.30pm) and no ads during play overnight; amended to start at least 15 minutes before; TV cap of three wagering ads per hour per channel 6am–8.30pm; radio ban 8–9am and 3–4pm on school days; ban on ads during children's programming/certain G programs; ban on uniform and sporting-venue advertising (existing deals grandfathered to 31 Dec 2031 unless extended/enlarged); ban on "notable persons" (athletes, ex-players, celebrities, influencers) promoting wagering; ban on promoting odds in broadcasts; content rules (no child/family-directed content, no linking with alcohol, responsible-gambling message required). - Levies: Interactive Gambling (Cost Recovery Levy) Bill 2026 lets ACMA recover costs of administering and enforcing Parts 7D/7E from licensed interactive wagering providers; National Self-exclusion Register (Cost Recovery Levy) Amendment Bill 2026 funds BetStop and a BetStop awareness campaign; amounts to be set by later ACMA instrument — not yet known. — [Senet](https://www.senetgroup.com/news-and-insights/australias-gambling-advertising-reforms); [iGaming Expert](https://igamingexpert.com/news/affiliates/australia-gambling-ads-reforms/)

Other: online keno and foreign-matched lotteries prohibited; trade-promotion exclusion narrowed (subscription/membership models like LMCT+ fall outside). — [Hall & Wilcox](https://hallandwilcox.com.au/news/proposed-wagering-advertising-reforms-new-restrictions-for-sport-media-and-online-platforms-in-australia/); [IMGL/Addisons](https://www.imgl.org/publications/imgl-magazine-volume-3-no-1/future-gambling-regulation-in-australia/)

- Political state: Coalition, Greens and crossbench said the package did not go far enough; Greens' Hanson-Young pushed a broad inducement ban; Kate Chaney's private member's Interactive Gambling Amendment (Stop the Gambling Ads) Bill 2026 (total ban) was introduced 30 Mar 2026 and remains "Before Reps" (not progressed). — [Parliament of Australia](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7459); [The Nightly](https://thenightly.com.au/politics/gambling-reform-deal-expect-to-pass-senate-as-parties-review-debate--c-22738574); [The New Daily](https://www.thenewdaily.com.au/news/politics/australian-politics/2026/05/13/gambling-reforms-albanese-government)

- The February 2026 statutory review of BetStop recommended more promotion, ongoing data matching, and expansion to online keno-type products. — [IMGL/Addisons](https://www.imgl.org/publications/imgl-magazine-volume-3-no-1/future-gambling-regulation-in-australia/)

### Inferences
- For a small entrant the operative federal risk is not advertising (small operators seldom buy broadcast/stadium inventory) but (i) the Part 7D inducement/red-flag monitoring regime, which demands systems to track customer behaviour and suppress offers, (ii) the affiliate-commission ban, which removes the revenue-share affiliate channel small books rely on for acquisition, and (iii) two new cost-recovery levies of unknown quantum.
- ACMA's enforcement pattern (Betfocus, LightningBet, TempleBet, BetChamps, Picklebet, Ultrabet, VicBet, Topbet, Buddybet) shows it investigates small/white-label brands routinely; sanctions on small operators have so far been warnings, remedial directions and undertakings (costly audits) rather than seven-figure fines, which have been reserved for Unibet, Dabble, Tabcorp.
- Because the Act grandfathers existing sponsorships to 2031 and exempts racing, the reform tilts the market toward racing-heavy and incumbent-sponsored brands.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the enacted Act text or legislation.gov.au commencement table; Royal Assent date (26 Aug 2026) rests on a third-party tracker.
- Final penalty amounts after amendment, the levy rates, and the "red flag" regulations are not yet published.
- No source found describing a federal duty-of-care statute or gambling ombudsman as enacted or formally proposed by the government in 2026.
- No source found for a federal national-licensing regime proposal that would replace state licences; the only such proposal was the Murphy recommendation, which the government rejected.

## Key Question 2 — AML/CTF: AUSTRAC obligations, the 2024 Amendment Act and 2026 changes, enforcement against wagering operators, and realistic cost for a one-person bookmaker on a platform

### Takeaway
Bookmakers were already tranche-1 reporting entities; the 2026 reforms (in force for existing entities from 31 March 2026) rewrite the AML/CTF program and customer due diligence framework, lower the initial-CDD threshold for certain gambling services from $10,000 to $5,000, and demand demonstrable, board-level, risk-based compliance — AUSTRAC has said a "purely procedural approach" is not enough. Enforcement in wagering has so far been aimed at large operators (Sportsbet, bet365, Entain), with Entain's Federal Court trial set for 30 November 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Reforms to the AML/CTF Act and the AML/CTF Rules 2025 took effect 31 March 2026 for existing reporting entities, including licensed gambling operators (other than lottery licensees), and 1 July 2026 for tranche-2 entities (lawyers, accountants, real estate, etc.); bookmakers are not tranche-2. — [Senet 2026 Gambling Outlook](https://www.senetgroup.com/news-and-insights/aml/2026-gambling-outlook); [Home Affairs overview](https://www.homeaffairs.gov.au/about-us/our-portfolios/criminal-justice/anti-money-laundering-and-counter-terrorism-financing/anti-money-laundering-and-counter-terrorism-financing-amendment-act/overview-of-the-aml-ctf-amendment-act)
- Program changes: no more Part A/Part B split; program must cover ML/TF/proliferation-financing risk assessment, risk-mitigation procedures and compliance measures; explicit requirement to appoint a "fit and proper" AML/CTF compliance officer; emphasis on governing-body/senior-management oversight; "independent review" replaced by independent evaluation. — [Mondaq/law-firm summary](https://www.mondaq.com/australia/financial-services/1619882/understanding-amlctf-reforms-part-1-existing-reporting-entities); [MinterEllison](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/the-countdown-to-commencement); [Senet 6 Oct 2026 via outlook page](https://www.senetgroup.com/news-and-insights/aml/2026-gambling-outlook)
- CDD: new initial CDD / ongoing CDD framework with simplified and enhanced tiers, PEP and sanctions screening; ongoing CDD under s 30 applies from 31 March 2026; a 3-year transition (31 Mar 2026–30 Mar 2029) lets existing entities keep using existing ACIP for onboarding; compliance-officer notification deadline extended to 30 May 2026; initial-CDD threshold for certain gambling services lowered from $10,000 to $5,000. — [Brightlaw transitional rules](https://www.brightlaw.com.au/?p=31355); [HSF Kramer, Feb 2026](https://www.hsfkramer.com/en_US/insights/2026-02/corporate-crime-360-your-complete-guide-to-apac-trends-in-2026/playing-by-new-rules-austracs-growing-reach-in-2026); [AGB 1 Apr 2026](https://asgam.com/2026/04/01/australias-new-aml-framework-kicks-in-today-placing-greater-responsibility-on-gambling-sector/)
- Tipping-off changes took effect earlier, 31 March 2025. — [Brightlaw](https://www.brightlaw.com.au/austrac-aml-reform-guidance-and-updates/)
- AUSTRAC CEO Brendan Thomas: gambling businesses pose an inherent ML risk; where serious non-compliance is found "we will apply for a civil penalty"; he criticised operators that failed to challenge high-value patrons depositing large sums without credible source-of-funds explanations, and flagged prepaid cards, payout cards, vouchers and overseas accounts as channels. — [AUSTRAC speech 2025](https://www.austrac.gov.au/news-and-media/speech/austrac-ceo-brendan-thomas-speaking-notes-regulating-game-2025); [AGB 11 Mar 2026](https://agbrief.com/news/11/03/2026/austrac-chief-calls-for-stronger-leadership-on-aml-as-reforms-loom/)
- Senet (29 Jul 2026): AUSTRAC has warned that "a purely procedural approach to compliance is not sufficient." — [Senet outlook page](https://www.senetgroup.com/news-and-insights/aml/2026-gambling-outlook)
- Privacy interaction: a small business (<$3m turnover) that is an AML/CTF reporting entity loses the Privacy Act small-business exemption for personal information handled for AML/CTF purposes (Privacy Act s 6E(1A)). — [HWL Ebsworth](https://hwlebsworth.com.au/small-businesses-big-change-privacy-obligations-under-tranche-2-of-the-aml-ctf-reforms/); [MinterEllison](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/aml-ctf-reforms-dont-forget-the-privacy-policy)

**Enforcement against wagering operators**
- Sportsbet: enforceable undertaking accepted May 2024 (risk assessment, customer monitoring, SMR deficiencies); AUSTRAC finalised it on 3 July 2026 after an external auditor confirmed remediation. — [AUSTRAC](https://www.austrac.gov.au/news-and-media/media-release/austrac-finalises-enforceable-undertaking-sportsbet)
- bet365: court-enforceable undertaking announced 6 July 2026 after gaps in ML risk assessment and suspicious-activity reporting (investigation launched 2024); must rebuild risk methodology and escalation; one report says completion required by July 2027. — [AUSTRAC](https://www.austrac.gov.au/news-and-media/media-release/bet365-overhaul-aml-systems-under-austrac-enforceable-undertaking); [AGB](https://asgam.com/2026/07/07/sports-betting-giant-bet365-enters-enforceable-undertaking-with-austrac-to-overhaul-aml-systems/)
- Entain (Ladbrokes/Neds): AUSTRAC filed Federal Court civil penalty proceedings 16 December 2024 alleging serious and systemic non-compliance; Entain provisioned A$100m (CEO says not an agreed figure); mediation held 30 July 2025; hearing listed from 30 November 2026 (two weeks). First test of the AML/CTF Act against an online betting provider. — [AUSTRAC](https://www.austrac.gov.au/news-and-media/media-release/austrac-takes-ladbrokes-and-neds-operator-entain-federal-court-over-serious-non-compliance-australias-money-laundering-laws); [The Straight](https://thestraight.com.au/court-date-set-in-late-2026-for-entain-austrac-case/); [The Straight (mediation)](https://thestraight.com.au/?p=28963)
- Context: Crown's $450m court-ordered penalty and SkyCity's $67m are the benchmark AML/CTF outcomes in gambling; Mounties (club) proceedings filed July 2025. — [AUSTRAC Mounties](https://www.austrac.gov.au/news-and-media/media-release/austrac-launches-civil-penalty-proceedings-against-pokies-giant-mounties); [AUSTRAC enforcement list](https://www.austrac.gov.au/lists-enforcement-actions-taken)

### Inferences
- There is no public AUSTRAC enforcement action against a small Australian bookmaker in 2022–2026 in the sources found; AUSTRAC's wagering enforcement has been exclusively against top-tier operators. The practical risk for a small entrant is AUSTRAC supervisory engagement, not litigation, provided a current (post-31 March 2026) program, named compliance officer, SMR/TTR reporting and ongoing CDD are in place.
- A platform provider can supply KYC tooling and transaction monitoring, but the reporting-entity obligations (enrolment, program, compliance officer, SMRs, TTRs, annual compliance report, independent evaluation) remain with the licensee; the "cannot outsource" principle ACMA applied to in-play applies equally under AML/CTF.
- Cost signal: the reforms' core new costs are a rewritten program/risk assessment (legal/consultant fees), compliance-officer "fit and proper" documentation, and periodic independent evaluation; no public figure for a micro-operator was found.

### Gaps
- AUSTRAC's own reform pages returned HTTP 503; findings rely on law-firm summaries. The final transitional Rules should be checked.
- No source quantifying AML/CTF compliance cost for a one-person bookmaker; no AUSTRAC enforcement example against a small bookmaker found.
- An unverified claim that AUSTRAC issued "compliance notices to three mid-tier operators" appeared in an unsourced article and is not relied on.
- The "$400 million" online-betting civil penalty figure referenced in a Lexology headline could not be verified (page blocked); Entain reporting does not mention it.

## Key Question 3 — Victoria: VGCCC enforcement 2023–2026, Victorian reforms, inducement ban, advertising restrictions, point-of-consumption tax, Tabcorp licence, minimum bet limits, and winning-punter account rules

### Takeaway
The VGCCC has fined or censured at least six small Victorian bookmaking partnerships since 2024 (MintBet $100k, Realbookie $50k, OkeBet $100k + censure, VicBet $130k, QuestBet $80k, Jimmy Bet censure), nearly all for Responsible Gambling Code of Conduct breaches (bonus bets to distressed customers, marketing to self-excluded people, sign-up inducements) — most are under VCAT review but OkeBet's was upheld in January 2026. ## Key Question 4 — Other states and territories: POCT rates, inducement and advertising rules binding an operator taking bets from residents

### Takeaway
Every jurisdiction except the NT levies a POCT (15% in NSW, SA, WA, Tas, Vic; 20% in Qld incl. racing levy; ACT reported 20%), each with its own threshold and reporting, and NSW's Betting and Racing Act inducement-advertising offence ($110,000 per offence for a corporation) is actively enforced by Liquor & Gaming NSW against small operators (SportChamps, BetDeluxe, Bet Right, PlayUp, betr).

## Key Question 5 — Data, privacy, cyber, consumer law and sports integrity obligations

### Takeaway
A small bookmaker is subject to the Privacy Act regardless of turnover because the small-business exemption is lost for AML/CTF-related personal information (and a wider second-tranche reform is in progress), must meet the Victorian Wagering and Betting Technical Standards (Tabcorp was fined $350,000 in Sept 2026 for missing mandatory MFA), and must hold sports controlling body integrity agreements before offering markets on each sport (Jimmy Bet censured Sept 2026 for not doing so).

### Cited Findings
- Privacy Act small-business exemption threshold is $3m turnover, but s 6E(1A) removes it for personal information a reporting entity handles for AML/CTF purposes; OAIC estimates >100,000 small businesses affected by the tranche-2 expansion; the AGD is progressing a second tranche targeting the blanket exemption and automated decision-making (exposure draft referenced; no commencement found). — [HWL Ebsworth](https://hwlebsworth.com.au/small-businesses-big-change-privacy-obligations-under-tranche-2-of-the-aml-ctf-reforms/); [MinterEllison](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/aml-ctf-reforms-dont-forget-the-privacy-policy); [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/australia-releases-exposure-draft-legislation-second/)
- Mandatory multi-factor authentication under the Victorian Wagering and Betting Technical Standards: Tabcorp fined $350,000 (24 Sep 2026) for failing to implement it between 30 Jan and 23 Jun 2025. — [VGCCC enforcement actions](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-community/hearings-decisions-and-appeals/decisions/enforcement-actions)
- Sports integrity: Jimmy Bet censured (16 Sep 2026) for offering betting on an approved event without the required sports controlling body integrity agreement; SCB agreements must provide for information sharing; VGCCC banned markets on under-19 competitions and under-18 players (2023). — [VGCCC enforcement actions](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-community/hearings-decisions-and-appeals/decisions/enforcement-actions); [VGCCC minors ban](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/media-release/vgccc-orders-ban-betting-minors-sport-events)

- Spam: ACMA has penalised spam non-compliance linked to BetStop integration and VIP messaging; VGCCC censured Tabcorp for 4,169 SMS without an opt-out link (licence condition 5.4 / Ministerial Direction S 349). — [Senet outlook](https://www.senetgroup.com/news-and-insights/aml/2026-gambling-outlook); [VGCCC enforcement actions](https://www.vgccc.vic.gov.au/for-community/hearings-decisions-and-appeals/decisions/enforcement-actions)

## Key Question 6 — Forward risks 2026–2028 and whether reform pushes small operators out

### Takeaway
Reform is now largely legislated and dated (1 Jan 2027 commencement, three-year phase-in, AdStop by proclamation, levies by ACMA instrument); the un-legislated risks are the quantum of the two federal levies and inducement regulations, any post-2027 Victorian POCT change, and the Entain AML/CTF judgment setting a penalty benchmark. The documented 2024–2026 wave of small-operator closures (Combet, Southern Cross Bet, Winbet, Betflux, Rambet, Punters Pal, Buddybet, BudgetBet, BoomBet) is attributed by trade press to regulation plus BetStop plus market contraction, not to any single rule.

### Cited Findings
- The Straight (22 Apr 2024): Punters Pal, Southern Cross Bet, Winbet (Victorian-licensed), Betflux, Rambet (NSW-licensed) and Combet all closed in early 2024; attributed to market contraction, "greater regulatory requirements, a tightening market plus the nationwide implementation of Betstop"; white-label platforms had lowered barriers during the pandemic boom; turnover thresholds let many avoid POCT; further consolidation expected. — [The Straight](https://thestraight.com.au/smaller-bookies-on-the-wane-as-punters-pal-joins-recent-closures/)
- Buddybet (peer-to-peer, Scott Didier) closed; BoomBet ceased services from 21 January 2026; BudgetBet (WA) surrendered its licence after going offline. — [The Straight Buddybet](https://thestraight.com.au/buddybet-joins-wsp-exodus); [The Straight 2026 preview](https://thestraight.com.au/?p=20564); [The Straight BudgetBet](https://thestraight.com.au/wa-regulators-left-to-sift-through-fallout-after-online-bookie-budgetbets-clo/)
- Industry commentator Gerard Daffy (Jul 2025) expects only a handful of operators to survive, arguing smaller operators rely on unsustainable sign-up offers and bonuses. — [SBC News](https://sbcnews.co.uk/sportsbook/2025/07/08/gerard-daffy-australia-bets/)
- The Straight's 2026 preview: "suggestions of further mergers and acquisitions continue to persist." — [The Straight](https://thestraight.com.au/?p=20564)
- Levy: the cost-recovery levy is described by trade press as "another compliance cost for Australia's licensed bookmakers," with size and structure dependent on final regulations. — [BeforeYouBet](https://www.beforeyoubet.com.au/australia-gambling-reform-advertising-opt-out-registry-2026)
- RWA (large operators) argued Victoria-specific inducement rules would roughly double the compliance burden if layered on federal changes. — [SiGMA](https://sigma.world/news/victoria-gambling-inducement-reforms/)
- Victorian budget cut gambling-tax forecasts and did not quantify the federal advertising reforms' effect on sports-wagering revenue. — [The Straight](https://thestraight.com.au/?p=25363)
- Addisons (IMGL, Jul 2026) expects operator exits and reduced NT/state revenue, continuing stakeholder disputes, and heavy dependence on federal–state cooperation; the NT, as de-facto national licensing home, bears the larger revenue impact. — [IMGL/Addisons](https://www.imgl.org/publications/imgl-magazine-volume-3-no-1/future-gambling-regulation-in-australia/)
- Offshore market: RWA estimates the illegal offshore market at A$3.9bn, growing to A$5bn by 2029. — [Senet outlook](https://www.senetgroup.com/news-and-insights/aml/2026-gambling-outlook)
- Entain's Federal Court hearing (30 Nov 2026) will produce the first judicial AML/CTF penalty benchmark for online wagering. — [The Straight](https://thestraight.com.au/court-date-set-in-late-2026-for-entain-austrac-case/)

### Inferences
- A national total advertising ban is unlikely before 2028: the government legislated restrictions in August 2026 with a three-year phase-in; Chaney's total-ban bill is stalled. Residual political risk is a tightening of inducement regulations under Part 7D (the Greens/crossbench position) rather than new primary legislation.
- Further POCT rises: Victoria's rate is legislated/published to 30 June 2027 and revenue is underperforming, so a post-2027 increase is possible but no proposal exists; NSW has resisted 20%; no 2026 Queensland change. The direction of travel since 2019 has been upward in every state.
- National licence/minimum capital: no live proposal found; the government rejected the national regulator, so state/NT licensing persists through 2028.
- For a small entrant the combined evidence suggests regulation is a manageable but rising fixed cost (program, audits, levies, SCB agreements, seven POCT registrations) whose real bite is the removal of the cheap acquisition tools small books depend on (sign-up offers, affiliate commissions, club sponsorship inducements) — the closures cited above are consistent with that mechanism.

### Gaps
- No Australian Bookmakers Association or Victorian Bookmakers Association submission on the 2026 reforms was found; industry positions available are RWA's (large operators) only.
- No government proposal for minimum capital or a national licence was found.
- The final levy rates, inducement regulations and AdStop start date are not yet published; these are the main unquantified forward costs.
