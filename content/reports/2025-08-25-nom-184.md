## Summary

I moved a motion at the 25 August 2025 Knox Council meeting about the increasing crime and community safety concerns in Rowville, Scoresby, and throughout the City of Knox.

Residents are telling me they’ve had enough. Some no longer feel safe doing the things they’ve always done. Older residents feel more vulnerable in their own homes. Parents are worried about their kids getting home safely.

The statistics are clear: youth crime in Knox has risen by almost half within a year. The stories I’m hearing from the Rowville and Scoresby communities are real, and the effects on crime victims can last a lifetime.

Families woken in the night by intruders. Cars stolen. Anti-social behaviour and violence. Home invasions, aggravated burglaries, criminal damage, assault, thefts from businesses, and drug- and alcohol-related crime.

I recognise the proactive efforts of Victoria Police’s Eastern Region Division 2 in establishing a new and vital Local Safety Committee that will bring together councillors, council officers, community agencies, and the Police to share relevant information, respond to emerging issues, and plan and coordinate crime prevention and community safety initiatives across Knox.

According to the Crime Statistics Agency (and the statistics updated since I spoke at the August Council meeting), crimes committed by children in Knox rose 48% in the past year, from 550 (June 2024) to 813 (June 2025). We are not alone in this – across Victoria, youth crime has surged to its highest level in more than 30 years.

Overall, there were 8,394 criminal incidents in Knox in the year ending June 2025, representing a 13% increase compared to the previous year.

Council is not trying to be the Police, but we must work more actively and closely with them for the benefit of our residents. I am dedicated to voicing and advocating for our community’s concerns about crime and safety, while making sure the Council supports the efforts of our hardworking and stretched local Police.
